# Corrections

This page lists errors on published pages. Nothing is removed from it.

## <span data-asof="2026-10-21">"Nationally in 2024"</span> — published 26 September 2026, corrected 21 October 2026

<span data-asof="2026-10-21">**What was wrong.** The methodology page said ActionSA polled {{hist_asa_npe_2024}} nationally in 2024. That is its share of Johannesburg's 2024 national ballot, not its national share.

**What is right.** The page says it polled {{hist_asa_npe_2024}} in the city in the 2024 national election.</span>

## <span data-asof="2026-10-21">"It is not estimated, projected or adjusted"</span> — published 26 September 2026, corrected 21 October 2026

<span data-asof="2026-10-21">**What was wrong.** The methodology page said the Electoral Commission publishes registration for each voting district and that the count "is not estimated, projected or adjusted". The forecast split 181 voting districts between wards using the Municipal Demarcation Board's figures.

**What is right.** The page says the forecast uses the Commission's count per ward, places each voting district in a single ward, and shares each ward's count among its voting districts.</span>

## <span data-asof="2026-10-17">"the roll carries about 560,000 registered white voters"</span> — published 26 September 2026, corrected 17 October 2026

<span data-asof="2026-10-17">**What was wrong.** The methodology page said the voters' roll carries about 560,000 registered white voters in Johannesburg, against about 300,000 white adults in the census. The roll does not record population group. The 560,000 was the model's own estimate, made with an adult share and a registration rate for each group fitted across the city, and it gave more registered white voters than there are white adults.

**What is right.** The page says the roll does not record population group and describes how the model estimates each group's registered voters. The comparison is removed.</span>

## <span data-asof="2026-10-07">"Going the other way — an ANC-led council built with ActionSA, the PA and the smaller parties instead — reaches it in 57%"</span> — published 26 September 2026, corrected 7 October 2026

<span data-asof="2026-10-07">**What was wrong.** The card on the ANC's claim to govern alone said an ANC-led council with ActionSA, the PA and the smaller parties reaches a majority in 57% of simulations. The red-lines card gave the same 57% and concluded it was "almost exactly the same odds as the DA's route" and that "on coalition arithmetic alone, neither leader is better placed than the other". The count left out the seats of the smallest parties.

**What is right.** Counting every party's seats, it reaches a majority in 78% of simulations, against 87% for a council without the ANC, the EFF and MK. The red-lines card no longer says the two are level.</span>

## <span data-asof="2026-10-07">"Without the ANC, the red line leaves a majority in 59% of simulations"</span> — published 6 October 2026, corrected 7 October 2026

<span data-asof="2026-10-07">**What was wrong.** The red-lines claim said a majority without the ANC, the EFF and MK exists in 59% of simulations, and called it more likely than not. The same 59% had been on the card since 26 September. The count left out the seats of the smallest parties.

**What is right.** Counting every party's seats, it exists in 87% of simulations: very likely.</span>

## <span data-asof="2026-10-07">"The DA can govern without the ANC, the EFF or MK"</span> — published 6 October 2026, corrected 7 October 2026

**What is right.** The claim is titled with what it tests: "A majority without the ANC, the EFF and MK". Her position is quoted in the card.</span>

## <span data-asof="2026-10-02">"The ANC is largest in 47% of simulations and the DA in 47%"</span> — published 26 September 2026, corrected 6 October 2026

<span data-asof="2026-10-02">**What was wrong.** The forecast page, the home page, "Nobody will win Johannesburg" and the review said the ANC and the DA were each the largest party in 47% of simulations. Where two parties tied for the most seats, the tie was counted as a win for one of them.

## "This is a deliberate choice, recorded, and it is the single largest reason the Johannesburg figure improves" — published 26 September 2026, corrected 27 September 2026

<span data-asof="2026-10-02">**What was wrong.** The about page reported how accurate the model was on the 2021 election, but the model had used party splits that happened after 2021. A forecast made before that election could not have known them. The page called this a deliberate choice.

**What is right.** The model uses only what was known before each election it is tested on, and the about page's accuracy figures come from that version.</span>
