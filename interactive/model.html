Play with the model

Untouched, this page shows the model's published forecast. Each control picks out the simulated elections that match your settings, so every answer stays within what the model drew. A control applies one mechanism to every party; its effect differs by party.

Figures labelled "computed in your browser from your settings" are worked out on your device: the site's re-weighting code runs in your browser on the model's published simulations, without re-running the model. They are not part of the published forecast and are not recorded. Your settings are kept only in the page's address, so a link carries them.

Polls

The published forecast uses no polls: its setting is zero trust. Why, and what polls were worth when that was measured, is under "Where polls fit" on the methodology page.

These are the Johannesburg polls the model's rules admit. At full trust each poll counts with the error the model computes for it alone: sampling error, a typical house error (the same for every house), a charge for an undisclosed likely-voter screen, and drift since fieldwork. A poll with no published sample size gets the typical house error in place of sampling error. This is narrower than the error the model's own poll blend uses, which prices the polls together. At half trust the error is doubled; at zero the poll has no weight. Full trust does not make the poll come true: the figures then describe the simulations nearest the polls.

Weight of recent by-elections

How much the model relies on the ward by-elections held since the last local election. Each setting is a separate full run of the model. The published weight is an unmeasured judgement, recorded in the model's register of judgement calls; only the November result can test it.

Computed in your browser from your settings.

By-elections are small, low-turnout contests, and some swings rest on a handful of wards. The swing column is measured in the wards that held by-elections only.

The model's column is each party's central level at this weight: the value its simulations are drawn around. Levels are not vote shares and do not add up to a whole, so compare a level only with the same party's level at the published weight, not with a share or with the swing column.

The three settings are separate runs, not the same simulations re-weighted, so part of any difference between them is sampling noise. A change in a party's middle seat count is marked "within sampling noise" when it is smaller than twice the combined standard error of the two settings' middle counts.
