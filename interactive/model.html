This page lets you alter turnout by population group, take into account a poll, and decide how much by-election results count. Untouched, it shows the published forecast.

Turnout by population group

Move any or all four. A group you leave alone stays at the forecast's turnout. The scale is percentage points of that group's registered voters.2

Where each party's votes come from

The model estimates this from the ward results of 2021 and the census population groups in each ward. The census reports population groups, so those are the groups the model can measure.3

A poll

Pick one poll, then how much to believe it.5

A poll's accuracy, as the model rates it, comes from its sample size, the polling house's typical miss and the time since fieldwork. At full belief the page gives a poll that error alone. The model's own blend of polls gives more, and caps the weight of one polling house.

Notes

  1. Your figures are worked out in your browser from the published simulations and are not recorded. A figure is withheld when its sampling error exceeds seats.
  2. The page does not re-run the model. It re-weights the model's {{n_draws}} published simulations towards those with the turnout you set. For Black African turnout at the top of the slider's range this was checked against a full re-run of the model, and every party's seat change agreed within sampling error. The other groups use the same method, unchecked. Past this range, or with groups moved far in opposite directions, the simulations run out and the slider stops.
  3. Each bar is one party's vote split by population group, as the model fits it to the ward results of 2021. "Registered" is the split of the voters' roll.
  5. Each poll is a Johannesburg subsample of a larger survey. Under a poll, the figures describe the model's simulations nearest the poll; the poll does not come true. The slider stops where too few simulations lie near the poll. That is a fact about the simulations, not a rating of the poll.
  6. By-elections are small, low-turnout contests, and some parties' swings rest on a handful of wards. The forecast counts them partly; the election will test that weight. Each setting is a separate full run of the model, so part of any difference between settings is sampling noise; the line under the control lists only changes larger than that.
  7. The Municipal Structures Act deducts the surplus. The other three are for comparison.
