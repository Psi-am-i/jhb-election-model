Break the model

Take the forecast above as your own. Call every ward, set each party's share of the vote, and pick who governs. The seats follow from the law that seats the council. Each ward starts at its most likely winner under your changes.

Your forecast, and the name you type when you share it, are kept only in the page's address, so a link carries them. Nothing is sent or stored.

Notes

  1. Your figures are worked out in your browser from the published simulations and are not recorded. A chance is the share of the {{n_draws}} simulations in which the outcome happens, weighted by your settings. A figure is withheld when its sampling error exceeds seats.
  2. The page does not re-run the model. It re-weights the model's {{n_draws}} published simulations towards those with the turnout you set. For Black African turnout at the top of the slider's range this was checked against a full re-run of the model, and every party's seat change agreed within sampling error. The other groups use the same method, unchecked. Past this range, or with groups moved far in opposite directions, the simulations run out and the slider stops.
  3. Each bar is one party's vote split by population group, as the model fits it to the ward results of 2021. "Registered" is the split of the voters' roll.
  5. Each poll is a Johannesburg subsample of a larger survey. Under a poll, the figures describe the model's simulations nearest the poll; the poll does not come true. The slider stops where too few simulations lie near the poll.
  6. By-elections are small, low-turnout contests, and some parties' swings rest on a handful of wards. The forecast counts them partly. Each setting is a separate full run of the model, so part of any difference between settings is sampling noise; the line under the control lists only changes larger than that.
  7. The Municipal Structures Act deducts the surplus. Germany's laws are here for comparison, and only a council seated under South African law can be shared. Breaking the model always seats by South African law.
  8. The vote in "Break the model" is the one the law counts: each party's share of the ward and list ballots together, always adding up to the whole. Moving one party scales the others in proportion. A party that wins a ward on your map always keeps a vote. The start shares give each party named in the forecast its middle seat count, or its ward count where it is called for more wards than that. The middles need not add up to the council's size: the seats left over go to the other parties, in proportion to their average seats in the simulations.
  9. "This outcome" is your council's: the party with the most seats on its own, or a tie for the most; whether any one party has a majority on its own; and, if you picked who governs, that coalition holding a majority. Its chance is the share of the published forecast's {{n_draws}} simulations, each counted once, in which all of these happen together. A ward's chance is the share in which your party wins it.
