Implementation review · City of Johannesburg 2026 LGE model

Review of the plan, the build, and the published forecast

An audit of the model against its own written plan: errors and implementation choices whose effect on published results is misleading or wrong, the assumptions a consumer of the forecast should know about and how to move them, and an assessment of the two-page distribution sheet. Part of the model's development record.

Reviewed 4 August 2026 Against joburg-prediction-model-plan-v2.md · METHODOLOGY.md · MODEL-LOG.md · src/ · forecast-sheet.html Verified on seat_draws.csv, 5,000 draws, seed 20261104

Addendum — second pass, later the same day: all findings resolved

Every error below is fixed, every adjustable assumption is now a user-facing control, and the plan items that were skippable-but-buildable have been built. The re-run changed the forecast materially, and the second pass verified the fixes rather than assuming them.

What the corrected model says. The dominant new finding is one the broken version could not see: overhang is the modal outcome (89% of draws). The ANC wins ~73 of 135 wards on a ~65-seat entitlement, the council expands to a median of ~281, and the majority threshold moves to ~141 — raising the bar for every coalition. ANC+DA clears it in 86.5% of draws and is the dominant minimal winning coalition; a DA-led minority via abstention (the 2021 pattern) is viable in 61%; the eight-party anti-ANC alliance is a coin flip at 53%; the DA is largest in only 63% of draws once overhang seats count. Banzhaf indices settle E1's asserted claim the other way: ActionSA (11%) out-ranks the PA (5%) as kingmaker. The A5 sensitivity was run and halves turnout leverage with rankings unchanged — the persuasion-not-mobilisation conclusion strengthens.

What was verified on the second pass. The seat allocator still reproduces all three published councils exactly; fold 2 was re-scored after the turnout-data fix (129/135 ward winners, blind seat error 60 — the γ-transfers/θ-doesn't finding strengthens); the interactive page's client engine was cross-checked headlessly against the Python run (DA 82 vs 81, threshold 140 vs 141, P(ANC+DA) 83.7% vs 86.5% — ward-resolution approximation, disclosed on the page); and a fresh bug found during that cross-check (the "widest field" probability computed over only the top-12 parties) was fixed before publication.

Still open, and honestly labelled: the Schedule 1 overhang fine print has no published IEC worked example (task #20 — now the forecast's largest legal uncertainty, bounded by the fixed-270 toggle); MK's γ cannot be fitted from one election and stays a documented default; the Census Small Area Layer (O7) still gates a better turnout covariate; and the citywide ranges remain judgements no backtest can validate — which is precisely why they are now sliders.

Deliverables: corrected src/montecarlo.py + new coalitions.py, byelections.py, gamma_recent.py, export_interactive.py/build_interactive.py, render_sheet.py; forecast-interactive.html (every assumption a bounded, defaulted control; scenario JSON round-trips to --config); regenerated forecast-sheet.html (rev 2, all figures generated from model outputs); MODEL-LOG §1.14–1.15 and METHODOLOGY updated. Original review preserved below as written.

Verdict in brief

The data work, the seat allocator, and the backtesting discipline are genuinely strong — the allocator reproducing three published councils exactly is a real credential, and several plan errors (softmax/logit, raw-versus-calibrated θ, entrant seeding) were caught and fixed well. But the Monte Carlo stage cut corners the plan explicitly warned against, and one of them makes the headline published conclusion factually wrong under the model's own output: a DA + EFF + MK coalition reaches a majority in 61% of the model's own draws, yet every published document says no such coalition exists. The two-pager repeats that error and separately overstates the validation. The fix for the worst finding costs nearly nothing — the per-draw seat vectors are already on disk.

Part 1

Errors whose effect on published results is misleading or wrong

E1

The coalition conclusion is false, because enumeration was pre-filtered on political plausibility

wrong result

Plan §3.8 was explicit: enumerate all 2ⁿ subsets, reduce to minimal winning coalitions, and "keep political feasibility as a separate, toggleable layer… There is no reason to pre-filter on plausibility, and good reason not to." The implementation did the opposite: montecarlo.py:60–67 hard-codes six politically plausible coalitions and checks only those. Running the full enumeration against the saved seat_draws.csv:

So the claim in MODEL-LOG §1.11, METHODOLOGY §5B, and the forecast sheet — "no two- or three-party coalition reaches 136 except ANC+DA" — is wrong. The sheet's page-2 lede, "a majority without the ANC exists on paper. It requires almost every other party in the chamber at once," is also wrong: three parties without the ANC clear it at the median. Even the standfirst's "no two-party majority available to anyone except the ANC and the DA" fails in about 4% of draws (DA+EFF).

What happened is a conflation of "the ANC" with "the ANC bloc": the code's everyone_else measures a majority excluding ANC and EFF and MK, and the narrative silently dropped the bloc qualifier. A DA+EFF arrangement may be politically outlandish — but the plan's whole §3.8 argument (Mashaba governed on external EFF support for three years; declared red lines have a short half-life) was that the arithmetic layer must never inherit that judgement. The correct statement is roughly: no majority exists without either the ANC or at least one of EFF/MK; the politically discussed anti-ANC coalitions — DA with ActionSA and the smaller parties — all fall short. That is still a strong finding, arguably a more interesting one: the DA's arithmetic paths all run through parties it has ruled out.

Also never built from §3.8: minimal-winning-coalition reduction, Banzhaf and Shapley–Shubik power indices, and the minority-government (plurality + abstention) outcome class. This matters for two published claims. The sheet asserts the PA "matters more to that arithmetic than ActionSA's recovery does" — precisely a Banzhaf claim, asserted rather than computed. And with the DA the largest party in 86% of draws, the historically realised outcome — a DA minority administration via abstention, as after 2021 — is missing as a reported class entirely.

Fix cost: near zero. No re-run needed — the per-draw seat vectors are saved in seat_draws.csv (top-12 parties, covering everyone that matters). Full enumeration, MWC reduction, and power indices over 12 parties are 4,096 subsets × 5,000 draws — seconds of compute, post-hoc.
E2

The Monte Carlo quietly discards the plan's per-party θ views inside the blocs

material

draw_target (montecarlo.py:122–124) centres the within-bloc Dirichlet on 2024 proportions. But the plan's §3.5 central θ values encode deliberate asymmetric views: θ_MK = 0.60 (no ward machinery, beaten by ActionSA in its one Soweto test, Ipsos at 8%), θ_ANC = 0.75, θ_EFF = 0.85. Centring on 2024 proportions flattens all of that to "everyone declines pro-rata," and with α = 60 the ANC-bloc split is tight, so MK's plan-central case (~7.3% citywide, ~20 seats) and its bear case (θ = 0.30, ~4%) are essentially unreachable — the Monte Carlo gives MK a median of 25 seats.

There is also an internal inconsistency in the plan that the implementation resolved silently: the θ modes imply an ANC-bloc shift of about −14.8 points (32.0×0.75 + 12.5×0.85 + 12.2×0.60 = 41.9 against a 56.7 base), while Δ_ANCbloc's default is −9. The parameter table contradicts itself, and the code picked the ANC-friendlier reading without flagging it. Knock-on: leverage.py:62–70 builds its "central scenario" from the θ modes, so the leverage analysis and the Monte Carlo are centred on different scenarios — DA+ActionSA at 113 seats in one and 105 in the other.

The plan's other guardrails here were also dropped: α (60 / 12) is hard-coded judgement where §3.5 says "set per bloc from polling dispersion," and the promise that per-party ranges "survive as sanity bounds on the derived values" is enforced nowhere.

How to adjust. In draw_target, centre the Dirichlet on θ-mode-adjusted proportions (centre = base_city[idx] * theta_mode[idx], renormalised) rather than raw 2024 shares; reconcile Δ_ANCbloc with the θ modes (a genuine judgement call, not a bug — decide which view is actually held); assert that derived θ falls inside §3.5's ranges and log the violation rate. Re-run montecarlo.py. Expected direction: ANC and MK each down several seats at the median, EFF up slightly, ANC+DA viability somewhat lower. The DA+EFF+MK number from E1 barely moves — EFF+MK combined is nearly invariant to the recentring.
E3

Ward winners and overhang are never computed in the forecast

material

Plan §3.7 steps 3–5: predict ward winners, deduct from entitlement, check for overhang and expand the council if it occurs — and the plan's own §0 flags overhang as "a live risk for 2026, not a theoretical one" (the ANC took 87 wards on a 91-seat entitlement in 2021). The Monte Carlo (montecarlo.py:202) allocates from combined votes with independent_wards=0, no_pr_list_wards=0 and never looks at wards. The machinery exists — fold.py aggregates VD predictions to ward winners, seats.py has overhang() — it was simply never wired into the forecast. If the DA wins suburban wards at a rate exceeding its ~87-seat entitlement (plausible given its ward-vote concentration and a fragmented field), the council expands and 136 stops being the threshold; none of the published probabilities account for that. Independents — newly permitted, and flagged in plan §6 — are dropped from the universe rather than carried as a C-deduction scenario.

How to adjust. Per draw: aggregate the ward-ballot VD prediction into 2026 wards via vd_ward_2026.csv, take FPTP winners, apply pr_seats()/overhang(), recompute the threshold when overhang occurs, and report P(overhang) by party. Re-run montecarlo.py. This also unlocks reporting which wards each party wins — an output the plan promised and campaigners would use.
E4

Three acquired evidence streams influence nothing

plan–build gap

Grepping the pipeline: by-elections (38 contests fetched; §3.6's exponential weighting, w_bye, and the §3.3 turnout-apathy covariate), polling (δ_p — the SRF-versus-Ipsos spread the plan calls "the honest input to δ_p's prior width"), and census covariates (the β term in λ̂) appear nowhere in fold.py, turnout.py, leverage.py, or montecarlo.py. δ is zero in the folds — defensibly, since fitting it there would be cheating — but the live case has no δ either. The forecast's citywide distribution rests entirely on four historical NPE→LGE transitions; everything from 2025–26 — Ward 82, Ward 130, Linden's turnout collapse, both polls — is narrative colour only. Meanwhile the sheet's colophon lists "IEC by-election results, 2021–2026" as a source, which is not true of the numbers on the sheet.

How to adjust. Either wire the evidence in — a modest version shifts each party's bloc/θ mode by w_bye × the §3.6-weighted by-election delta — or state prominently that the forecast is priors-only and correct the colophon. This is the largest silent divergence from the plan's intent: the §8 anchor apparatus was built precisely so current evidence would move the forecast.
E5

The validation claim on the sheet overstates what was validated

misleading

The sheet says: "Tested against 2021 without being shown the answer, the model called 128 of 135 ward winners." Two problems. The reproduce command seeds --entrant ASA=0.1812 — ActionSA's actual 2021 citywide result — and the headline seat-error row (12) uses θ recalibrated against the actual 2021 citywide shares. The genuinely blind configuration (θ and γ both carried from fold 1) had a total seat error of 54. The internal documents are honest about this distinction; the sheet is not.

The deeper point, worth one sentence in METHODOLOGY §5B: what fold 2 validates is the spatial projection machinery (γ and the logit-deviation model) — which is the right thing to validate, since the Monte Carlo also conditions on a drawn citywide target. But it means all of the forecast's uncertainty lives in the drawn citywide distributions, and nothing in the backtests validates those ranges. The published probabilities (87.5%, 0.0%, …) are exactly as good as §3.5's hand-set priors, no better.

How to adjust. Rephrase the sheet: "shown only the citywide totals, the model placed them across wards well enough to call 128 of 135 ward winners." Add the priors-carry-the-uncertainty sentence to §5B.
E6

Minor parties move in lockstep, and one parameter was invented

distorted intervals

A single shared f_other draw scales the IFP, VF+, ACDP, Rise and the rest identically per draw (montecarlo.py:129–134). This produces the implausibly tight published intervals — Rise 5–7 seats, IFP 4–6; a party's 90% interval spanning two seats is not credible — and overstates correlation in every coalition sum containing minors, including the 99.9% for "every party outside the ANC bloc." Separately, ALJAMAAH's triangular (0.80, 1.20, 1.80) at montecarlo.py:53 appears in no plan table — an invented parameter that should be documented in §3.5 or the log.

How to adjust. Draw each modelled minor independently with wider ranges, and consider an LGE-specific collapse risk for suburbs-NPE parties (Rise, BOSA). Re-run. Direction: minor-party intervals widen substantially; the non-ANC-bloc field's 99.9% drops.
Part 2

Assumptions and choices to surface — and how to move them

These are defensible but consequential. Each should be stated wherever results are presented; most have a cheap sensitivity run.

A1

Ward/PR split-ticket ratios frozen at 2021

adjustable

Ratios are measured from 2021, clipped to [0.5, 2.0], and default to 1.0 for parties absent in 2021 (montecarlo.py:175–181). MK — a list-vote party the plan itself says has "no ward machinery" — gets ratio 1.0, overstating its ward votes and hence its combined entitlement. Contestation coverage (plan §0: "a 2026 forecast must take a view on how many wards each party fields") is unmodelled; the PA's 2021 ratio bakes in its 52-of-135-ward contestation, which it will likely exceed in 2026.

Adjust: set MK's ratio near ActionSA's 0.77, or draw it 0.6–1.0; scale the PA's ratio up for wider contestation. Re-run montecarlo.py. Direction: MK entitlement down, PA up — this interacts directly with E1's DA+EFF+MK number.
A2

No turnout uncertainty in the Monte Carlo

adjustable

One fixed weight vector serves all 5,000 draws (montecarlo.py:166). Justified by the fold finding that turnout is second-order (~6 seats of total error), but six seats is not nothing next to coalition thresholds, and holding it fixed narrows every interval on the sheet.

Adjust: per draw, blend the λ̂-projected pattern with the 2021-level pattern under a random weight, plus VD-level noise scaled to the observed λ residual spread. Direction: all seat ranges widen modestly; probabilities near 0 or 1 soften.
A3

The 2026 turnout projection uses the specification that lost its own head-to-head

adjustable

turnout.py projects T_2026 = T_2024 × λ̂ — the ratio form — although the model's own test found the previous-LGE-level form better in both measurable cycles (MAE 0.0409 against 0.0592). The fold defence (consistency of weighting matters more than specification) covers seats, but ward winners and the leverage geography consume this pattern too.

Adjust: add a --spec level option projecting T_2026(i) from T_2021(i) rescaled to the λ̂ citywide level; regenerate turnout.csv, then re-run leverage.py and montecarlo.py. Direction: mostly moves ward-level outputs, not citywide seats.
A4

γ defaults to 1.0 for every party fold 1 never saw

adjustable

That is ActionSA, MK, and effectively the PA: the three most decision-relevant uncertain parties receive an unfitted "preserve 2024 geography exactly" assumption, while the fitted-γ story ("γ transfers across cycles") is told about established parties. ActionSA's Ward 130 by-election — three times its 2024 level in Soweto — suggests its geography is actively shifting.

Adjust: fit γ_ASA and γ_PA on the 2021→2024 transition (both parties existed in both), or run the Monte Carlo at γ = 0.8 for the unfitted three as a sensitivity.
A5

The plan's A5 sensitivity check was never run, and the leverage scale is a choice

adjustable

Plan §5 promised a re-run of leverage with marginal voters drawn from a 50/50 blend of VD shares and the ward's non-voter demographics. leverage.py inherits A5 and notes it, but the check does not exist — and the reversed strategic conclusion ("turnout cannot close the gap") leans on A5. The census ward data acquired for exactly this purpose is unused. Also worth stating: ±5pp per ward is a chosen scale; 2021 showed differential swings far larger. The honest phrasing is "the most extreme differential-turnout scenarios tested are worth single-digit seats against a 31-seat gap," not "two seats."

Adjust: implement the 50/50 blend using the ward-level census product; add a scenario at ±10–15pp differential across half the city as an outer bound.
A6

New-entrant blindness has an unused mitigation

adjustable

R7 is well documented, but the --entrant mechanism is unused in the live case. A generic "unknown 2026 entrant" slot — drawing, say, 0–8% citywide in some fraction of draws — reflects what actually happened in three consecutive elections (EFF era, ActionSA, MK) and would honestly widen the tails instead of leaving the risk as a footnote.

Adjust: add the entrant slot to draw_target; report results with and without.

Smaller notes: MODEL-LOG has duplicate section numbers (two §1.9s, two §1.10s); montecarlo.py renormalises the drawn target, so drawn bloc shifts are not exactly preserved marginally; turnout.py:166 weights the 2021 prediction test by 2024 registration rather than 2021's (harmless, but inconsistent); the sheet's numbers are hand-copied into the HTML, duplicating coalition_viability.csv, and will silently go stale after any re-run.

Part 3

What is solid and worth defending

  • The seat allocator validated exactly against three published councils — quota, total valid votes, and every party's seat count — across two council sizes.
  • The combined-ballot entitlement correction, with the ActionSA split-ticket counter-example that proves it matters.
  • Catching the softmax/logit inconsistency and the raw-versus-calibrated θ trap — both would have silently corrupted results by multiple points.
  • Registration-weighted ward apportionment, including the 59 zero-voter slivers that area weighting would have miscounted.
  • The γ-transfers-and-θ-doesn't finding — the model's best single insight, and the correct justification for its architecture.
  • Testing the boundary-stability assumption to immateriality instead of hoping.
  • The documentation culture: stating predictions in advance and reporting when they fail is rarer than it should be.
Part 4

The two-page distribution sheet

The sheet is light for a specific reason: it presents conclusions while withholding the two ideas that make the model credible. What is missing is not length — it is the argument.

Required fixes (accuracy, not polish)

  • The page-2 lede and any "no two/three-party coalition except ANC+DA" phrasing must change per E1. Suggested honest version: "Every viable majority runs through either the ANC or the EFF/MK bloc. The coalitions actually being discussed — DA with ActionSA and the smaller parties — top out around 116 seats in nineteen simulations out of twenty."
  • The hero stat's "the only pairing that can" → "the only pairing that reliably can."
  • "Tested against 2021 without being shown the answer" → "shown only the citywide totals, the model placed them across wards well enough to call 128 of 135 ward winners."
  • Drop by-elections from the sources line, or wire them in (E4).
  • Add the missing caveat — the plan's own line is perfect: the model says which arithmetic is available, not whether two people who dislike each other will sit in the same room. Without it, "87.5%" will be read as the probability of an ANC–DA government, which it is not.

The compelling-explanation gap: four beats the sheet has earned but does not tell

  1. Why seats, not votes. Johannesburg's formula adds both ballots and allocates compensatorily — winning wards does not add seats — so a forecast must model the whole formula, not poll shares. Hook: the allocator reproduces the real 2011, 2016 and 2021 councils to the seat.
  2. Why geography anchors it. Where a party's support sits is stable between elections even when its level swings wildly — tested across cycles, the geography transferred and the level did not. So the model fits geography and treats levels as explicit scenarios. This one finding justifies the entire architecture and is currently absent from the sheet.
  3. Why parties are drawn in blocs. The DA and ActionSA fight over the same voters, so their errors offset — which is why their combined range (91–116) is tight while ActionSA alone spans 5–38. The most counterintuitive numbers on the sheet, presented without their explanation.
  4. Why turnout is not the story. The model was built expecting turnout to decide the election, tested that belief, and reversed it. Already the sheet's best section — it needs the "we expected the opposite" framing made explicit, which is what makes it credible.

A short "why believe this / why not" pairing — validated allocator and 128/135 ward winners on one side, blind-to-new-entrants and priors-not-polls on the other — would complete it. Structurally there is room: page 1's lower third is one paragraph, and the "How it works" note is three generic sentences that could become the four beats above.

Recommendation

Suggested order of operations

  1. Enumeration, MWC reduction, power indices (E1) — free, post-hoc on seat_draws.csv, and it changes the headline claim. Do first.
  2. Dirichlet recentring (E2) and the ward-winner/overhang step (E3) — each changes the numbers; one re-run each.
  3. Sensitivities from Part 2 as time allows, A1 and A2 first.
  4. Re-issue the sheet with the Part 4 restructure, generating its numbers from the CSVs rather than by hand.

Hold distribution of the current sheet. E1 is the kind of error a competent reader will find, and it is the headline.
