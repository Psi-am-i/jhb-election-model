8 August 2026: Published
Forecast · City of Johannesburg · 4 November 2026

Nobody will win Johannesburg — and an obscure voting law will give the ANC more seats than expected. Get ready for squabbles.

5,000 simulations using a new voting-district model of the city find the DA finishing first 62%↘ of the time — but never with a majority. A rarely-used clause in our voting laws will hand the shrinking ANC an unbreakable floor: it will keep its ward seats, even though this is more than its proportional allowance — and the shortfall will be taken from every other party.

29 September 2026: what the model says now

The largest party is a near tie, where on 8 August the DA led clearly: 47% chance it's the ANC alone and 45% chance it's the DA alone. Two or more parties tie for the most seats: 1.4% chance. A single party reaches a majority on its own: 0.1% chance. The ANC still triggers the excessive-seats clause: 62% chance. A chance is the share of the 5,000 simulations in which the outcome happens.

The rule that shapes everything

Wards, lists — and the November surprise

Johannesburg votes on two ballots at once: one elects a ward councillor, race by race — highest total wins, no majority needed, no runoff — and one is for a party. It seems like they elect two separate halves of the council, but they don't — the two ballots are added together, and that combined share sets the total seats each party is entitled to. Winning a ward doesn't add a seat; it fills one the party was already owed, and the party lists top everyone up to their entitlement.

The quirk: a party with tightly concentrated support can win more wards than its overall share entitles it to. Nobody unseats a ward winner — the seat law's "excessive seats" clause (Schedule 1, item 16, completed by a 2021 amendment) settles it: the winner keeps every ward, receives no list seats at all, and the other parties' list seats are recalculated over what remains. The council stays at 270 and the majority at 136 — we found the Electoral Commission applying exactly this in Laingsburg in 2021. It is now the ANC's normal condition: roughly 74↘ stronghold wards on a 65↘-seat entitlement, in 96%↘ of simulations. Its ward count is an unbreakable floor — and the parties squeezed to pay for it are everyone else.

29 September 2026: what the model says now

Still the ANC's usual condition, and weaker than published: about 60 wards on a 58-seat entitlement. The clause fires for the ANC: 62% chance. When it does not, the ANC also gets list seats.
