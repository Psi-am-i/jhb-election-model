The DA cannot win Joburg without SowetoQUITE LIKELY

No party is likely to win a majority on its own: under 0.1% chance. So winning Joburg means the DA leading a coalition of at least 136 of 270 seats. Zille has ruled out the EFF and MK. That leaves three realistic partners: ActionSA with the PA, or the ANC. In 2021 the DA won no Soweto ward and governed only through a 9-party coalition that fell within a year. So what about 2026?

  1. Mathematically, the DA can lead a coalition in 92% of simulations. Most of these depend on many parties staying together, any of which can walk out and bring the coalition down. We do not count them.
  2. In over 99% of simulations the DA wins none of the 41 Soweto wards. Without a Soweto ward it can still govern in 27% of simulations, with ActionSA and the PA or with the ANC: 17% and 19% respectively (some simulations allow either).
  3. In the 0.2% of simulations where the DA does win a Soweto ward, its chance of governing jumps to 80%. These simulations show a strong DA across the whole city: the breakthrough the DA has never achieved, and needs to make inroads.
  4. All told, Mashaba is probably correct. The DA needs Sowetan support, or partners who have it.
  5. What this cannot see. The model counts seats, not which parties are willing to work together.
21 October 2026◆: what the model says now

Since then the forecast reads the IEC's September roll, which adds a ward to Soweto: 41 wards. The DA wins none of them in over 99% of simulations. Without one it governs in 27%: with ActionSA and the PA in 17%, with the ANC in 19%. The claim holds with a 73% chance, which the scale calls quite likely.
