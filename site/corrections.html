This page lists errors on published pages. Nothing is removed from it.

“The whole model beats simple baselines” — published 6 October 2026, corrected 25 October 2026

What was wrong. The methodology’s scoring section, and the pages quoting it, presented This Model’s scores against past elections as a test of forecasting each election from earlier ones. Several of its settings had been chosen on records that include the elections scored.

What is right. The scores are withdrawn. Re-scored figures, from earlier elections only, will be published on the methodology page.

“The model uses only what was known before each election it is tested on” — published 2 October 2026, corrected 25 October 2026

What was wrong. That overclaimed, because several scores on previous elections had been inadvertently using data that included those same elections.

What is right. This Model’s 2026 forecast uses only what was known before the 2026 election. Its scores on past elections are withdrawn; re-scored figures, for earlier elections, will be published on the methodology page.

“forecasting that election from earlier ones” — published 22 October 2026, corrected 23 October 2026

What was wrong. The tooltips on the home page’s ward counts said This Model called each past election’s ward winners “forecasting that election from earlier ones”. Several of its settings were chosen on records that include those elections.

What is right. The ward counts are withdrawn with This Model’s other scores on past elections.

“which adds a ward to Soweto” — published 21 October 2026, corrected 23 October 2026

What was wrong. The card on Herman Mashaba’s Soweto claim said the IEC’s September roll adds a ward to Soweto. No ward was added: ward 29 crossed this site’s rule for a Soweto ward once this site placed each voting district wholly in one ward.

What is right. The card says the forecast reads the IEC’s September roll and that this site counts that many wards as Soweto, and a dated note on it says why ward 29 now counts.

“checked against a full re-run of the model, and every party’s seat change agreed within sampling error” — published 21 October 2026, corrected 23 October 2026

What was wrong. A note on Your prediction said moving Black African turnout had been checked against a full re-run of This Model and agreed. That check was made on the model before the one published on 21 October, and it does not hold for that one.

What is right. The note says the page re-weights This Model’s published simulations towards the turnout set, and that it shows those simulations, not what This Model forecasts if turnout changes.

“Parties that compete for the same voters trade support” — published 21 October 2026, corrected 23 October 2026

What was wrong. The forecast page and the methodology said parties that compete for the same voters trade support, so when one rises the others tend to fall. The model published on 21 October draws no such trade: the DA and ActionSA rise and fall together across its simulations.

What is right. The sentence is removed.

“Part of each simulation’s move is shared by all parties” — published 21 October 2026, corrected 23 October 2026

What was wrong. The methodology said part of each simulation’s move is shared by all parties. Parties with a fifth of the vote or more share none of it.

What is right. It says parties below a fifth of the vote share part of each simulation’s move, and a party with a fifth or more moves on its own.

“your settings are not recorded” — published 14 October 2026, corrected 23 October 2026

What was wrong. Your prediction said a reader’s settings are not recorded. Opening the share sheet with a setting moved seals the prediction and stores it as an entry.

What is right. The note says a reader’s settings are not stored until the share sheet is opened with a setting moved, which seals and enters the prediction, and links the privacy page.

“The whole rest of the council except the ANC” — published 9 October 2026, corrected 23 October 2026

What was wrong. The card on Helen Zille’s red lines described the first route to a majority as the whole rest of the council except the ANC. Its figure leaves out the ANC, the EFF and MK together.

What is right. A dated note on the card says so.

“Inside Metros” — published 9 October 2026, corrected 23 October 2026

What was wrong. The card on Herman Mashaba’s Soweto claim named the outlet as Inside Metros. The report is Inside Politics’, of an interview with Inside Metros.

What is right. The card names Inside Politics, and a dated note on it says why.

“grey seats are too close to call” — published 7 October 2026, corrected 23 October 2026

What was wrong. The share image of our forecast said its grey seats are too close to call. They are the seats the parties’ middle counts leave unfilled.

What is right. It says grey is the seats the middles leave over.

“The only records the site keeps are those described above, held by Cloudflare for the site” — published 6 October 2026, corrected 23 October 2026

What was wrong. The privacy page named only Cloudflare. Your prediction loads Pyodide and numpy from jsDelivr, which receives readers’ IP addresses and browser details. On results night the entries are also copied to the site author’s computer to rank them.

What is right. The page names jsDelivr and links its policy, says the results-night copy is kept until the entries are deleted, and says that opening a shared link checks its seal and stores nothing.

“136 are needed to govern” — published 6 October 2026, corrected 23 October 2026

What was wrong. The portal’s Johannesburg card said 136 seats are needed to govern. 136 is a majority; a minority administration can govern with fewer.

What is right. It says 136 is a majority.

“Your forecast applies three versions of Germany’s seat law instead” — published 6 October 2026, corrected 23 October 2026

What was wrong. The methodology said Your prediction applies Germany’s seat laws instead of South Africa’s. Your prediction seats every council under South African law and shows Germany’s for comparison.

What is right. It says so.

“Fifteen Johannesburg wards have held by-elections since 2021” — published 6 October 2026, corrected 23 October 2026

What was wrong. The sources page said fifteen wards had held by-elections. Fifteen by-elections were held, in fourteen wards: ward 102 held two.

What is right. It says 15 by-elections have been held in 14 Johannesburg wards since 2021.

“its one realistic option is ActionSA and the PA” — published 6 October 2026, corrected 23 October 2026

What was wrong. The forecast page said that if the DA rules out the ANC, the EFF and MK, its one realistic option is ActionSA and the PA. With the smaller parties as well, the DA has a majority far more often, as the card on Helen Zille’s red lines shows.

What is right. The page gives both chances: ActionSA and the PA alone, and with the smaller parties.

“Each source file is listed with its checksum” — published 6 October 2026, corrected 23 October 2026

What was wrong. The home page said the sources page lists each file with its checksum. The checksums are in the project’s repository, not on the page.

What is right. It says the checksums are in the project’s repository.

“home language by ward, which Stats SA holds but does not publish in the ward product; we have requested it” — published 26 September 2026, corrected 23 October 2026

What was wrong. The methodology said Stats SA holds home language by ward and had been asked for it, and that income, employment and dwelling type are request-only because at ward level they can identify households. The sources page said the same of small-area data. Stats SA wrote in August 2026 that Census 2022 has none of these below municipality level. The methodology also said population group is the only ward-level characteristic Stats SA publishes; it publishes age and sex as well.

What is right. Both pages say Census 2022 publishes home language, income, employment and dwelling type only down to municipality level, and that Stats SA confirmed none exists by ward. Population group is named as the one of the three ward characteristics that separates groups of voters.

“her party took about one-eighteenth as much of the DA’s vote in Johannesburg as it did in Cape Town” — published 26 September 2026, corrected 23 October 2026

What was wrong. The methodology said GOOD took about one-eighteenth as much of the DA’s vote in Johannesburg as in Cape Town. The record gives about one-sixth.

What is right. It says about one-sixth.

“Only one such split had happened anywhere before 2021” — published 26 September 2026, corrected 23 October 2026

What was wrong. The methodology said only one split led from the leader’s own city had happened before 2021. The record holds two: GOOD in Cape Town and the NFP in eThekwini.

What is right. It names the two.

“In one city the model loses. Uniform swing beats it in Mangaung, the smallest council in the set” — published 26 September 2026, corrected 23 October 2026

What was wrong. The methodology said This Model loses in one city, Mangaung, the smallest council. Buffalo City’s council is smaller. On seats given to the wrong party This Model also loses in eThekwini and Cape Town, and ties in Mangaung and Nelson Mandela Bay.

What is right. The sentence is generated from the scores and names the cities where This Model loses on each measure.

“Every party in this table existed in 2021” — published 26 September 2026, corrected 23 October 2026

What was wrong. The methodology said every party in the backtest’s tables existed in 2021. ActionSA first stood in 2021 and is This Model’s largest miss there.

What is right. It says so.

“The seat columns judge only the single most likely outcome” — published 26 September 2026, corrected 23 October 2026

What was wrong. The methodology said its seat columns judge the single most likely outcome. They judge each party’s middle forecast. Johannesburg’s error was also printed both as seats given to the wrong party and as twice that figure, as seats out.

What is right. The columns are described as each party’s middle forecast, and every seat error is given as seats given to the wrong party.

“take the national swing since the last election and apply it equally everywhere” — published 26 September 2026, corrected 23 October 2026

What was wrong. The methodology described the uniform-swing baseline as the national swing since the last election. It adds each party’s change between the city’s two national elections before the one forecast to the previous local result.

What is right. It describes that.

“pre-2021 polling missed ActionSA in this city by about ten points” — published 26 September 2026, corrected 23 October 2026

What was wrong. The methodology compared two polls as if both were of the city; one was a subsample of all the metros. It also said pre-2021 polling missed ActionSA in the city by about ten points. The only independent poll was national; scaled to the areas ActionSA contested it implied about 6.3%, against 18.12%.

What is right. It says which poll covered what, and gives those two figures.

“A city appears here with numbers only once its allocator reproduces that city’s published 2011, 2016 and 2021 councils exactly” — published 26 September 2026, corrected 23 October 2026

What was wrong. The portal said a city shows numbers only once its seat calculation reproduces its published councils. Every metro’s already does; what a city lacks is a 2026 forecast. The other metros’ seat and ward counts were not labelled as 2021’s.

What is right. It says a city shows numbers once it has a 2026 forecast, and labels the other metros’ counts as 2021’s.

“the council growing beyond 270” — published 8 August 2026, corrected 23 October 2026

What was wrong. The review said counting ward winners detects overhang, “the council growing beyond 270”. Under the law the council stays at 270: a party that wins more wards than its vote entitles it to keeps them and gets no list seats, and the other parties share the remaining seats.

What is right. It says that.

“never been demonstrated in a published calculation by the electoral commission” — published 8 August 2026, corrected 23 October 2026

What was wrong. The review said the fine print of the excessive-seats clause had never been demonstrated in a published calculation by the Electoral Commission. The Commission’s published calculation for Laingsburg in 2021 applies it.

What is right. It says the clause has not been tested in court, and that the Commission’s Laingsburg calculation applies it.

“Largest party in 63% of simulations” — published 8 August 2026, corrected 23 October 2026

What was wrong. The review said the corrected forecast had the DA as largest party in 63% of simulations. The run it describes gave 61%.

What is right. It says 61%.

“a DA-led minority government resting on abstention (viable in 61% of simulations)” — published 8 August 2026, corrected 23 October 2026

What was wrong. The review gave 61% for a DA-led minority government. That was the figure under a council that grows; under the law it was 53%.

What is right. It says 53%.

“In roughly six of seven simulations, the ANC wins more wards than its shrunken vote entitles it to” — published 8 August 2026, corrected 23 October 2026

What was wrong. The review said this happened in roughly six of seven simulations. The forecast published with it put it at 96%.

What is right. It says most simulations.

“district-year records where more people had voted than were registered” — published 8 August 2026, corrected 23 October 2026

What was wrong. The review said the dropped records were ones where more people had voted than were registered. They were records with turnout above 105% of registered voters.

What is right. It says that.

“it is how the DA took this mayoralty in 2021” — published 8 August 2026, corrected 23 October 2026

What was wrong. “Minority administrations” said abstention installs minority governments and that this is how the DA took Johannesburg’s mayoralty in 2021. Mpho Phalatse won 144 votes to 121, a majority of the whole council. The table’s row “DA minority, only ANC opposes (2021 pattern)”, on the page from 26 September, was wrong for the same reason.

What is right. The piece says the mayor needs a majority of the votes cast, so councillors who stay out lower the bar, and gives the 2021 vote as 144 to 121. The row reads “DA minority, only the ANC opposes”.

“the shortfall will be taken from every other party” — published 8 August 2026, corrected 23 October 2026

What was wrong. “Nobody will win Johannesburg”, its listing and “Laingsburg, 2021” said the shortfall is taken from every other party. The list seats that remain are re-divided among the other parties; in Laingsburg one party lost a seat.

What is right. They say the other parties share the list seats that remain.

“it has fired exactly once” — published 8 August 2026, corrected 23 October 2026

What was wrong. “Laingsburg, 2021” and the listings of it and of “Nobody will win Johannesburg” said the clause has fired exactly once. About 65 of the Commission’s seat calculations were searched, and Laingsburg is the only case found.

What is right. They say it is the only case found in the Commission’s seat calculations we searched.

“on just 23% of the vote” — published 8 August 2026, corrected 23 October 2026

What was wrong. “Laingsburg, 2021” and the methodology gave the DA’s share in Laingsburg as 23%. The Commission’s calculation gives 1,530 of 6,453 votes.

What is right. They give 24%.

“Germany 2000” — published 8 August 2026, corrected 23 October 2026

What was wrong. “If the law were German instead” and Your prediction named Germany’s seat laws “Germany 2000” and “Germany 2015”. Germany passed no seat law in either year: surplus seats went uncompensated until 2013 and were levelled from the 2013 law.

What is right. They read “Germany before 2013”, “Germany 2013 law” and “Germany 2023 law”, and the piece’s caption describes them in the table’s order.

“The over-winner stays slightly over-represented; the cost falls on everyone else” — published 8 August 2026, corrected 23 October 2026

What was wrong. The methodology said the over-winner stays slightly over-represented and the council is always close to proportional. The over-winner keeps every ward and can hold far more seats than its vote entitles it to.

What is right. It says that, and that the shortfall comes off the other parties’ list seats.

“every other party combined sheds about 190,000 votes” — published 8 August 2026, corrected 23 October 2026

What was wrong. The card on Helen Zille’s 490,000 claim said that with the total frozen, every other party would shed about 190,000 votes. The other parties lose what the DA gains: about 250,000.

What is right. A dated note on the card gives 248,674.

“Nationally in 2024” — published 26 September 2026, corrected 21 October 2026

What was wrong. The methodology page said ActionSA polled 6.22% nationally in 2024. That is its share of Johannesburg’s 2024 national ballot, not its national share.

What is right. ActionSA polled 6.22% in the city in the 2024 national election.

“It is not estimated, projected or adjusted” — published 26 September 2026, corrected 21 October 2026

What was wrong. The methodology page said the Electoral Commission publishes registration for each voting district and that the count “is not estimated, projected or adjusted”. The forecast split 181 voting districts between wards using the Municipal Demarcation Board’s figures.

What is right. The forecast uses the Commission’s count per ward, places each voting district in a single ward, and shares each ward’s count among its voting districts.

“the roll carries about 560,000 registered white voters” — published 26 September 2026, corrected 17 October 2026

What was wrong. The methodology page said the voters’ roll carries about 560,000 registered white voters in Johannesburg, against about 300,000 white adults in the census. The roll does not record population group.

What is right. The comparison is removed.

“Going the other way — an ANC-led council built with ActionSA, the PA and the smaller parties instead — reaches it in 57%” — published 26 September 2026, corrected 9 October 2026

What was wrong. The card on the ANC’s claim to govern alone said an ANC-led council with ActionSA, the PA and the smaller parties reaches a majority in 57% of simulations. The red-lines card gave the same 57% and concluded it was “almost exactly the same odds as the DA’s route” and that “on coalition arithmetic alone, neither leader is better placed than the other”. The count left out the seats of the smallest parties.

What is right. Counting every party’s seats, it reaches a majority in 78% of simulations, against 87% for a council without the ANC, the EFF and MK.

“Without the ANC, the red line leaves a majority in 59% of simulations” — published 6 October 2026, corrected 9 October 2026

What was wrong. The red-lines claim said a majority without the ANC, the EFF and MK exists in 59% of simulations, and called it more likely than not. The same 59% had been on the card since 26 September. The count left out the seats of the smallest parties.

What is right. Counting every party’s seats, it exists in 87% of simulations: very likely.

“The DA can govern without the ANC, the EFF or MK” — published 6 October 2026, corrected 9 October 2026

What was wrong. The tested claim from Helen Zille was titled “The DA can govern without the ANC, the EFF or MK”. She ruled out a coalition with the EFF and MK; she did not rule out the ANC.

What is right. The claim is titled with what it tests: “A majority without the ANC, the EFF and MK”. Her position is quoted in the card.

“The ANC is largest in 47% of simulations and the DA in 47%” — published 26 September 2026, corrected 6 October 2026

What was wrong. The forecast page, the home page, “Nobody will win Johannesburg” and the review said the ANC and the DA were each the largest party in 47% of simulations. Where two parties tied for the most seats, the tie was counted as a win for one of them.

What is right. The ANC alone has the most seats in 47% of simulations and the DA alone in 45%. In 1.4%, two or more parties tie for the most seats.

“Without the ANC, the red line holds about half the time” — published 26 September 2026, corrected 6 October 2026

What was wrong. The red-lines claim on the forecast page said that without the ANC the red line holds “about half the time”, and called it a coin flip. The figure was 59% of simulations, which is not half.

What is right. Without the ANC, the red line leaves a majority in 59% of simulations. The verdict is “MORE LIKELY THAN NOT”.

“The 587,000 votes the ANC shed between 2016 and 2021” — published 26 September 2026, corrected 6 October 2026

What was wrong. The home page said the ANC shed 587,000 votes between 2016 and 2021.

What is right. The ANC bloc lost 587,000 votes between the 2019 national election and the 2021 local election, as the article says.

“Where on 31 August the DA led clearly” — published 26 September 2026, corrected 6 October 2026

What was wrong. A note at the top of “Nobody will win Johannesburg” said the piece was published on 31 August.

What is right. It was published on 8 August.

“The most reliable majority in the city that does not involve the EFF or MK” — published 26 September 2026, corrected 6 October 2026

What was wrong. We called the DA and the ANC “the most reliable majority in the city that does not involve the EFF or MK”. Several majorities without the EFF or MK occur in most simulations.

What is right. The DA and the ANC are the most reliable two-party majority in the city.

“A government can be formed without the EFF or MK” — published 26 September 2026, corrected 6 October 2026

What was wrong. A tested claim was titled “A government can be formed without the EFF or MK”. What it tested was narrower: whether a council without the ANC, the EFF and MK has a majority. The quote was dated 11 September.

What is right. The claim is titled “The DA can govern without the ANC, the EFF or MK”. The quote is dated 13 September, the date of the report it comes from.

“18 September 2026: Published” on three tested claims — published 26 September 2026, corrected 6 October 2026

What was wrong. Three tested claims on the forecast page were dated 18 September.

What is right. They were first published on 26 September.

“This is a deliberate choice, recorded, and it is the single largest reason the Johannesburg figure improves” — published 26 September 2026, corrected 27 September 2026

What was wrong. The about page reported how accurate This Model was on the 2021 election, but This Model had used party splits that happened after 2021. A forecast made before that election could not have known them. The page called this a deliberate choice.

What is right. This Model no longer uses party splits that happened after the election it is tested on.

Four figures on “Nobody will win Johannesburg” labelled “published 31 August 2026” — published 26 September 2026, corrected 27 September 2026

What was wrong. Four figures on that piece were labelled as published on 31 August 2026. The piece was published on 8 August, with different figures. A sentence beside them read “Still the ANC’s usual condition, and a stronger one”.

What is right. The four figures are the ones published on 8 August, and the sentence reads “Still the ANC’s usual condition, and weaker than published”.
