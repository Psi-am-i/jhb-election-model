Forecast · City of Johannesburg · 4 November 2026

Nobody will win Johannesburg — and an obscure voting law will give the ANC more seats than expected. Get ready for squabbles.

5,000 simulations using a new voting-district model of the city find the DA finishing first 71%↘ of the time — but never with a majority. A rarely-used clause in our voting laws will hand the shrinking ANC an unbreakable floor: it will keep its ward seats, even though this is more than its proportional allowance — and the shortfall will be taken from every other party.

23 September 2026: what the model says now

The DA is the largest party in 47% of the 5,000 simulations and the ANC in 47% — a near tie, where on 31 August the DA led clearly. A single party reaches a majority on its own in under 0.1% of them. The ANC still triggers the excessive-seats clause in 65%.

Commentary
The rule that shapes everything

Wards, lists — and the November surprise
31 August 2026: Published

Johannesburg votes on two ballots at once: one elects a ward councillor, race by race — highest total wins, no majority needed, no runoff — and one is for a party. It seems like they elect two separate halves of the council, but they don't — the two ballots are added together, and that combined share sets the total seats each party is entitled to. Winning a ward doesn't add a seat; it fills one the party was already owed, and the party lists top everyone up to their entitlement.

The quirk: a party with tightly concentrated support can win more wards than its overall share entitles it to. Nobody unseats a ward winner — the seat law's "excessive seats" clause (Schedule 1, item 16, completed by a 2021 amendment) settles it: the winner keeps every ward, receives no list seats at all, and the other parties' list seats are recalculated over what remains. The council stays at 270 and the majority at 136 — we found the Electoral Commission applying exactly this in Laingsburg in 2021. It is now the ANC's normal condition: roughly 55↗ stronghold wards on a 54↗-seat entitlement, in 58%↗ of simulations. Its ward count is an unbreakable floor — and the parties squeezed to pay for it are everyone else.

23 September 2026: what the model says now

Still the ANC's usual condition, and a stronger one: about 61 wards on a 58-seat entitlement, with the clause firing in 65% of the 5,000 simulations. In the rest the ANC gets list seats as well.

The precedent — a dress rehearsal in the Karoo

Laingsburg, 2021: the whole mechanism in seven seats
31 August 2026: Published

This clause is not a theory — it has fired exactly once, in a Karoo town with a seven-seat council. In 2021 the DA won three of Laingsburg's four wards on just 23% of the vote, a share entitling it to only two seats. The Electoral Commission's published calculation shows what the law does next: the DA kept all three wards and received no list seats; its votes were set aside and a fresh quota computed for everyone else over the four seats that remained; and the small Karoo Democratic Force lost the seat it would otherwise have held. The council stayed at seven. Scale that up thirty-eight-fold and you have this forecast's central case: the ANC winning roughly 55↗ of Johannesburg's wards on a 54↗-seat entitlement — keeping every one, taking zero list seats, the squeeze recalculated onto everyone else inside a council fixed at 270. A quirk that has fired once, in one small town, is about to govern the country's biggest metro.

23 September 2026: what the model says now

The central case now has the ANC on about 61 wards against a 58-seat entitlement; the clause fires in 65% of the 5,000 simulations.

The counterfactual — four answers to one problem

Other Proportional Representation Mechanisms

South Africa is not the only democracy whose voters hand a party more constituency wins than its proportional share. Germany faced the same arithmetic for decades — and answered it differently three times. Until 2013 the Bundestag simply grew: the winner kept its extra seats and everyone else kept their proportional count (Überhangmandate). Modern Germany went further and added levelling seats (Ausgleichsmandate) for everyone else, growing the chamber until full proportionality was restored. In 2023 Germany abolished overhang entirely: a constituency winner whose party lacks the list share is now simply not seated. South Africa's 2021 amendment chose the opposite approach: the council size does not change, the winner keeps what it won, and the other parties lose some representation.

The counter-intuitive part

When the ANC falls, nobody catches the votes
31 August 2026: Published

Most voters assume the ANC's decline must hand victory to someone else. The record says otherwise. Between 2019 and 2021 — the ANC bloc's biggest collapse ever — it shed 587,000 votes across Johannesburg's voting districts. The opposition captured essentially none of them: the DA's bloc actually lost 52,000 votes of its own over the same stretch, even with ActionSA arriving from nowhere to take 18%. The opposition's share surged only because ANC voters stayed home and the total shrank. The voting blocs behave like separate reservoirs whose levels rise and fall — not pipes into each other.

That is why the DA can finish first without ever nearing a majority, and why "the ANC is collapsing" and "someone else must be winning" are entirely different claims. If 2026 breaks this pattern — and the boldest July polling would require it to — it would be the first time in the city's democratic history.

23 September 2026: what the model says now

Our earlier model grouped voters into ANC- and DA-leaning blocs. It now uses pools of voters defined by census data and by how they previously voted. The finding holds, but is formulated differently.

The third way to govern

Minority administrations

No coalition table shows it, but the mayor is elected by councillors present and voting, so abstention installs minority governments — it is how the DA took this mayoralty in 2021. A minority administration is viable when its candidate outpolls the parties that actively vote against it, with everyone else abstaining. In the 5,000 simulations, that route is as open to the ANC as to the DA:

About the model
Before the numbers

How to read this page

This forecast is not one prediction — it is 5,000 simulations of the election, each drawing a plausible 2026 within the bounds of everything observed since 2006. Every percentage on this page means the same thing: the share of those 5,000 simulations in which something happens. "89%" never means a vote share or a poll number — it means the thing occurred in about 4,450 of the 5,000 runs. The ranges beside each seat count are where the middle 90% of simulations landed.

Limits

What this forecast cannot tell you
  • Viability is not intent. The 44% for ANC+DA is arithmetic, not a prediction that it happens. The model says which deals are available; it says nothing about whether people who dislike each other will sit in the same room.
  • A new party is sized from history, not from polling. Every party on the IEC's certified ballot is named in the simulations, and a party with no record is sized from how past newcomers performed. ActionSA went from absent to 44 seats in one cycle — a breakthrough of that kind is exactly what this sizing can underestimate.
  • The ranges are the output. A single seat number quoted from this sheet without its range is a misuse of it. The shares of simulations are exactly as good as the stated assumptions, which are set out on the methodology page.
  • The excessive-seats law: settled by precedent, confirmation pending. We located both the 2021 amendment that completes Schedule 1 and the Electoral Commission's own Laingsburg 2021 calculation applying it; the Commission has been asked to confirm our reading.
  • This is not a poll. No voters were surveyed. This is arithmetic on results, registration, and 15 by-elections. Polls are not an input: we keep them only as an outside check on the model.
The campaign arithmetic

The parties' claims, tested

Public, checkable statements about November — each one put through the same 5,000 simulations behind everything above.

7 August 2026: Published
Democratic AllianceHelen Zille, DA · Business Day, March 2026
FALSE
We need 490,000 registered DA voters to go vote DA, if they do that we win an outright majority”

When we put the claim through the full model on 7 August 2026 it fell short by 39 seats at the median. The figures below are from that run. The model has changed since, and the turnout setting they were measured under no longer exists, so read them as history rather than as today's forecast. The claim also falls at its use of arithmetic.

  1. It is half of the wrong number. 490,000 is almost exactly half of the ~985,000 votes expected in Johannesburg if turnout stays at its record low. At first glance it looks plausible, until you realise that if there are more voters, you need half of a bigger number. If all DA members did what Helen wanted, the total number of voters would be ~1,290,000, and half of that is 644,000 — not 490,000.
  2. They can't reach the higher number. Push every DA and ActionSA supporter to the highest turnout their areas have ever recorded — 75%, a turnout fantasy at a local election — and the DA does clear the wrong original target, polling ~524,000. But that is still well short of 644,000.
  3. Even 41% of the vote doesn't buy 41% of the seats. 41% would be 110 seats of 270. But the excessive-seats law still fires in nine simulations out of ten, handing the ANC every stronghold ward it holds — about 62 wards on an entitlement of about 50. The surplus is deducted from everyone else's column, the biggest slice from the biggest party. So the DA's median actually lands on 97 of the 136 needed.
  4. Could she still be right? We looked for a reading that works. Her exact words — “We need 490,000 registered DA voters to go vote DA, if they do that we win an outright majority” — never say what total she is measuring against. Against 2021's poll of ~917,000 ballots, 490,000 would be 53%. So the claim is true in exactly one world: the DA doubles its vote while every other party combined sheds about 190,000 votes, keeping the voter numbers frozen at the record low. If that happened, is Helen right? Nothing in twenty years of turnout records produces that world — but even assuming it exists, while the ANC holds its ~62-ward wall the excessive-seats law means the DA needs about 57% of the vote to reach 136. So even in a special universe with never-before-seen events, it is false. The few simulations where the DA does govern alone are the ones where its surge breaks the wall itself, flipping stronghold wards — in those draws the ANC falls to ~47 seats. To do that, the DA needs to do more than turn out its existing supporters. It needs new ones.
23 September 2026: what the model says now

The turnout setting this test used no longer exists in the model, so the surge cannot be re-run as it was. In today's forecast, with no surge at all, the DA wins a majority on its own in under 0.1% of the 5,000 simulations — 1 of them. 1 of those take at least 30 of the ANC's usual wards from it, with the ANC falling to 34–34 seats. The other route to a DA majority — the ANC holding its wards while ActionSA and the EFF collapse — no longer appears in any simulation. The claim's verdict stands; the scenario that makes a DA majority possible is further from turnout alone than it was.

18 September 2026: Published
African National CongressDickson Masemola, ANC NEC member · Joburg ETC, 13 August 2026
OUT OF REACH
We want to restore this city. But we can only be able to do so if the people of greater Johannesburg give their organisation, the ANC, absolute authority without any hesitation”

This is an appeal, not a forecast, so the thing to test is whether the mandate being asked for is available at all. Absolute authority means governing without needing anyone else: 136 of the 270 seats. In the 5,000 simulations behind today's forecast, the ANC does it in 0 of them.

  1. Not once, and not close. The ANC's median is 67 seats. Its single best result anywhere in the 5,000 simulations is 128 — still 8 short of 136, and that draw is the outer edge of the range, not a likely outcome.
  2. Being first is a different question, and that one is open. The ANC is the largest party in 47% of simulations against the DA's 47%. First place is a genuine contest. Sole control is not: no party reaches 136 alone in more than a handful of draws.
  3. The ANC does have routes — they are all coalitions. With the EFF and MK, its closest partners on this ballot, the three together reach 136 in 12% of simulations, on a median of 116 seats. Going the other way — an ANC-led council built with ActionSA, the PA and the smaller parties instead — reaches it in 57%. These routes and others are open to the ANC. Absolute authority is not.
18 September 2026: Published
uMkhonto weSizwe PartyBongani Baloyi, MK Party mayoral candidate for Johannesburg · TimesLive, 9 September 2026
VERY UNLIKELY
We firmly believe that these polls are underpolling MK Party, just as they did in the 2024 elections. According to us, we are going to be the biggest party in the city”

Unlike most campaign talk this is a precise, checkable prediction: more seats than anyone else. MK is the largest party in 1.4% of the 5,000 simulations.

  1. The gap is large, not marginal. MK's median is 21 seats of 270. Even at the top of its range — 50 seats, a result it beats in only one simulation in twenty — it is short of the ANC's typical 67 and the DA's 66.
  2. First place is a two-horse race in this forecast. The ANC is largest in 47% of simulations and the DA in 47%. Everything else, MK included, shares what is left.
  3. The complaint about polls does not reach this forecast. Our model uses no poll at all. It is built from the record — how this city has actually voted, and the trajectories we can measure in that record. So the claim that pollsters undercount MK, whether or not it is right, leaves these numbers where they are. To be largest, MK would have to be under-represented in past ballot boxes, not in a survey — a different and much harder argument.
18 September 2026: Published
Democratic AllianceHelen Zille, DA · Daily Maverick, 13 September 2026
A COIN FLIP
We won't go into a coalition with MK, and we will not go into coalition with the EFF. Because our value set is too incompatible. You cannot govern with people who don't share any of your ideology”

A red line is a choice, not a claim about the world, so there is nothing here to call true or false. What can be tested is what it costs. Ruling out the EFF and MK leaves the DA two high-probability ways to a majority: everyone else in the council except the ANC, which clears 136 in 59% of simulations, or a deal with the ANC itself, which clears it in 44%. The narrower option — ActionSA and the PA alone — is the 17% case discussed just below.

  1. Without the ANC, the red line holds about half the time. A council built from every party except the ANC, the EFF and MK reaches 136 in 59% of the 5,000 simulations. That is the whole of the rest of the council acting as one — the most generous possible reading of the DA's options, and it still comes down to a coin flip.
  2. The plausible version of that coalition is much weaker. The DA with ActionSA and the Patriotic Alliance — the partners most often named — reaches 136 in 17% of simulations, on a median of 118 seats. Getting from there to a majority means adding parties that will each hold a handful of seats, and keeping all of them.
  3. The alternative the red line leaves is a deal with the ANC. The DA and the ANC together clear 136 in 44% of simulations. Neither party has said it wants that. It is, on this arithmetic, the most reliable majority in the city that does not involve the EFF or MK.
  4. The same test applied to the other side. An ANC-led council excluding the DA, the EFF and MK reaches 136 in 57% of simulations — almost exactly the same odds as the DA's route. On coalition arithmetic alone, neither leader is better placed than the other.
