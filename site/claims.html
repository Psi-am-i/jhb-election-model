The campaign arithmetic

The parties' claims, tested

Each claim below is a public, checkable statement about November, tested against the same 5,000 simulations as the forecast.

7 August 2026: Published
Democratic AllianceHelen Zille, DA · Business Day, March 2026
FALSE
We need 490,000 registered DA voters to go vote DA, if they do that we win an outright majority”

When we put the claim through the full model on 7 August 2026 it fell short by 39 seats at the median. The figures below are from that run. The model has changed since, and the turnout setting they were measured under no longer exists, so read them as history rather than as today's forecast. The claim also falls at its use of arithmetic.

  1. It is half of the wrong number. 490,000 is almost exactly half of the ~985,000 votes expected in Johannesburg if turnout stays at its record low. At first glance it looks plausible, until you realise that if there are more voters, you need half of a bigger number. If all DA members did what Helen wanted, the total number of voters would be ~1,290,000, and half of that is 644,000 — not 490,000.
  2. They can't reach the higher number. Push every DA and ActionSA supporter to the highest turnout their areas have ever recorded — 75%, a turnout fantasy at a local election — and the DA does clear the wrong original target, polling ~524,000. But that is still well short of 644,000.
  3. Even 41% of the vote doesn't buy 41% of the seats. 41% would be 110 seats of 270. But the excessive-seats law still fires in nine simulations out of ten, handing the ANC every stronghold ward it holds — about 62 wards on an entitlement of about 50. The surplus is deducted from everyone else's column, the biggest slice from the biggest party. So the DA's median actually lands on 97 of the 136 needed.
  4. Could she still be right? We looked for a reading that works. Her exact words — “We need 490,000 registered DA voters to go vote DA, if they do that we win an outright majority” — never say what total she is measuring against. Against 2021's poll of ~917,000 ballots, 490,000 would be 53%. So the claim is true in exactly one world: the DA doubles its vote while every other party combined sheds about 190,000 votes, keeping the voter numbers frozen at the record low. If that happened, is Helen right? Nothing in twenty years of turnout records produces that world — but even assuming it exists, while the ANC holds its ~62-ward wall the excessive-seats law means the DA needs about 57% of the vote to reach 136. So even in a special universe with never-before-seen events, it is false. The few simulations where the DA does govern alone are the ones where its surge breaks the wall itself, flipping stronghold wards — in those draws the ANC falls to ~47 seats. To do that, the DA needs to do more than turn out its existing supporters. It needs new ones.
29 September 2026◆: what the model says now

The turnout setting this test used is no longer in the model, so the surge cannot be re-run. In today's forecast, with no surge, the DA wins a majority on its own: 0.1%▲ chance (3▲ of the 5,000 simulations). 3▲ of those take at least 30 of the ANC's usual wards from it, and the ANC falls to 21▼–52 seats. The other route to a DA majority, the ANC holding its wards while ActionSA and the EFF collapse, no longer appears in any simulation. The verdict stands. A DA majority depends less on turnout than it did.

26 September 2026: Published
African National CongressDickson Masemola, ANC NEC member · Joburg ETC, 13 August 2026
OUT OF REACH
We want to restore this city. But we can only be able to do so if the people of greater Johannesburg give their organisation, the ANC, absolute authority without any hesitation”

This is an appeal, not a forecast, so the thing to test is whether the mandate being asked for is available at all. Absolute authority means governing without needing anyone else: 136 of the 270 seats. In the 5,000 simulations behind today's forecast, the ANC does it in 1▲ of them.

  1. Not close. The ANC's median is 66▼ seats, against the 136 it would need. Its single best result anywhere in the 5,000 simulations is 144▲, and that draw is the outer edge of the range, not a likely outcome.
  2. Being first is a different question, and that one is open. The ANC alone has the most seats in 47% of simulations, the DA alone in 45%▼, and two or more parties tie for the most seats in 1.4%. First place is a genuine contest. Sole control is not: no party reaches 136 alone in more than a handful of draws.
  3. The ANC does have routes — they are all coalitions. With the EFF and MK, its closest partners on this ballot, the three together reach 136 in 12% of simulations, on a median of 116 seats. Going the other way — an ANC-led council built with ActionSA, the PA and the smaller parties instead — reaches it in 57%. These routes and others are open to the ANC. Absolute authority is not.
29 September 2026◆: what the model says now

The ANC reaches a majority on its own: under 0.1% chance (1▲ of the 5,000 simulations), with 144▲ seats at its best. When this claim was first written it did so in none of them.

26 September 2026: Published
uMkhonto weSizwe PartyBongani Baloyi, MK Party mayoral candidate for Johannesburg · TimesLive, 9 September 2026
VERY UNLIKELY
We firmly believe that these polls are underpolling MK Party, just as they did in the 2024 elections. According to us, we are going to be the biggest party in the city”

Unlike most campaign talk this is a precise, checkable prediction: more seats than anyone else. MK is the largest party in 1.4% of the 5,000 simulations.

  1. The gap is large, not marginal. MK's median is 22▲ seats of 270. Even at the top of its range — 50 seats, a result it beats in only one simulation in twenty — it is short of the ANC's typical 66▼ and the DA's 66.
  2. First place is a two-horse race in this forecast. The ANC alone has the most seats in 47% of simulations and the DA alone in 45%▼. Two or more parties tie for the most seats in 1.4%. Everything else, MK included, shares what is left.
  3. The complaint about polls does not reach this forecast. Our model uses no poll at all. It is built from the record — how this city has actually voted, and the trajectories we can measure in that record. So the claim that pollsters undercount MK, whether or not it is right, leaves these numbers where they are. To be largest, MK would have to be under-represented in past ballot boxes, not in a survey — a different and much harder argument.
26 September 2026: Published
Democratic AllianceHelen Zille, DA · Daily Maverick, 13 September 2026
MORE LIKELY THAN NOT
We won't go into a coalition with MK, and we will not go into coalition with the EFF. Because our value set is too incompatible. You cannot govern with people who don't share any of your ideology”

A red line is a choice, not a claim about the world, so there is nothing here to call true or false. What can be tested is what it costs. Ruling out the EFF and MK leaves the DA two high-probability ways to a majority: everyone else in the council except the ANC, which clears 136 in 59% of simulations, or a deal with the ANC itself, which clears it in 43%▼. The narrower option — ActionSA and the PA alone — is the 16%▼ case discussed just below.

  1. Without the ANC, the red line leaves a majority in 59% of simulations. A council built from every party except the ANC, the EFF and MK reaches 136 in that share of the 5,000 simulations. That is the whole of the rest of the council acting as one — the most generous possible reading of the DA's options.
  2. The plausible version of that coalition is much weaker. The DA with ActionSA and the Patriotic Alliance — the partners most often named — reaches 136 in 16%▼ of simulations. Two medians describe them and they answer different questions: across all 5,000 simulations the three hold 117▼ seats, and in the 16%▼ where they do reach a majority they hold 143▼. The who-can-govern table reports the second, because it is asking what governing looks like when it happens. Getting from the first to a majority means adding parties that will each hold a handful of seats, and keeping all of them.
  3. The alternative the red line leaves is a deal with the ANC. The DA and the ANC together clear 136 in 43%▼ of simulations. Neither party has said it wants that. It is, on this arithmetic, the most reliable two-party majority in the city.
  4. The same test applied to the other side. An ANC-led council excluding the DA, the EFF and MK reaches 136 in 57% of simulations — almost exactly the same odds as the DA's route. On coalition arithmetic alone, neither leader is better placed than the other.
