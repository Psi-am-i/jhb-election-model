The campaign arithmetic

The parties' claims, tested

Each claim below is a public statement about November's result by a named party figure, tested against the forecast's 5,000 simulations, except where a card says its test used an earlier run of This Model. We choose statements the simulations can check, such as a seat count, a majority, the largest party or a coalition's arithmetic, and look for them from every party. Know of one? Send it to psi@whysoserious.city.

7 August 2026: Published · Link to this claim
Democratic AllianceHelen Zille, DA · Business Day, March 2026
We need 490,000 registered DA voters to go vote DA, if they do that we win an outright majority”
490,000 DA voters would win the DA an outright majorityFALSE

When we put the claim through This Model on 7 August 2026 it fell short by 39 seats at the median. The figures below are from that run. This Model has changed since, and the turnout setting they were measured under no longer exists, so read them as history rather than as today's forecast. The claim also falls at its use of arithmetic.

  1. It is half of the wrong number. 490,000 is almost exactly half of the ~985,000 votes expected in Johannesburg if turnout stays at its record low. If there are more voters, you need half of a bigger number. If all DA members did what Zille wanted, the total number of voters would be ~1,290,000, and half of that is 644,000 — not 490,000.
  2. They can't reach the higher number. Push every DA and ActionSA supporter to the highest turnout their areas have ever recorded, 75%, and the DA does clear the wrong original target, polling ~524,000. But that is still well short of 644,000.
  3. Even 41% of the vote doesn't buy 41% of the seats. 41% would be 110 seats of 270. But the excessive-seats law still fires in nine simulations out of ten, handing the ANC every stronghold ward it holds — about 62 wards on an entitlement of about 50. The surplus is deducted from everyone else's column, the biggest slice from the biggest party. So the DA's median lands on 97 of the 136 needed.
  4. Could she still be right? We looked for a reading that works. Her exact words — “We need 490,000 registered DA voters to go vote DA, if they do that we win an outright majority” — never say what total she is measuring against. Against 2021's poll of ~917,000 ballots, 490,000 would be 53%. So the claim is true in exactly one world: the DA doubles its vote while every other party combined sheds about 190,000 votes, keeping the voter numbers frozen at the record low. If that happened, is Zille right? Nothing in twenty years of turnout records produces that world — but even assuming it exists, while the ANC holds its ~62-ward wall the excessive-seats law means the DA needs about 57% of the vote to reach 136. So even in that world, it is false. The few simulations where the DA does govern alone are the ones where its surge breaks the wall itself, flipping stronghold wards — in those simulations the ANC falls to ~47 seats. To do that, the DA needs to do more than turn out its existing supporters. It needs new ones.
23 October 2026: what This Model says now

The turnout setting this test used is no longer in This Model, so the surge cannot be re-run. In the current forecast, the DA wins a majority on its own in none of the 5,000 simulations. The verdict stands. On 8 October Zille repeated the claim, saying "136 seats out of 270" (News24, 9 October 2026). Correction, 23 October 2026: step 4 said every other party combined sheds about 190,000 votes. With the total frozen, the other parties lose what the DA gains: about 248,674. On 20 October Zille said: "I will repeat this until I am blue in the face: 490 000 people need to vote DA on both ballots. That would give us 136 of the council's 270 seats." (News24, 20 October 2026, interviewed by Ann Bernstein of CDE.) This Model expects a median of 1,013,994 votes on each ballot, so 490,000 is 48%. The DA reaches 490,000 on both ballots in 0 of 5,000 simulations. If the DA did win 48% that would take some of the ANC's wards.

26 September 2026: Published · Link to this claim
African National CongressDickson Masemola, ANC NEC member · Joburg ETC, 13 August 2026
We want to restore this city. But we can only be able to do so if the people of greater Johannesburg give their organisation, the ANC, absolute authority without any hesitation”
The ANC can govern Johannesburg alone againNEAR IMPOSSIBLE

This is an appeal, not a forecast, so the thing to test is whether the mandate being asked for is available at all. Absolute authority means governing without needing anyone else: 136 of the 270 seats. In the 5,000 simulations behind today's forecast, the ANC does it in 0 of them.

  1. The ANC's median is 72 seats, against the 136 it would need. Its single best result anywhere in the 5,000 simulations is 98, and that simulation is the outer edge of the range, not a likely outcome.
  2. Being first is a different question, and that one is open. The ANC alone has the most seats in 45% of simulations, the DA alone in 47%, and two or more parties tie for the most seats in 4.0%. First place is a genuine contest. Sole control is not: no party reaches 136 alone in more than a handful of simulations.
  3. The ANC does have routes — they are all coalitions. With the EFF and MK, its closest partners on this ballot, the three together reach 136 in 11% of simulations, on a median of 120 seats. Going the other way — an ANC-led council built with ActionSA, the PA and the smaller parties instead — reaches it in 78%. These routes and others are open to the ANC. Absolute authority is not.
23 October 2026: what This Model says now

Since then the ANC reaches a majority on its own in none of the 5,000 simulations. Its best result is 98 seats.

26 September 2026: Published · Link to this claim
uMkhonto weSizwe PartyBongani Baloyi, MK Party mayoral candidate for Johannesburg · TimesLive, 9 September 2026
We firmly believe that these polls are underpolling MK Party, just as they did in the 2024 elections. According to us, we are going to be the biggest party in the city”
MK will be the biggest party in the cityEXTREMELY UNLIKELY

Unlike most campaign talk this is a precise, checkable prediction: more seats than anyone else. MK is the largest party in 3.7% of the 5,000 simulations.

  1. The gap is large, not marginal. MK's median is 19 seats of 270. Even at the top of its range — 57 seats, a result it beats in only one simulation in twenty — it is short of the ANC's typical 72 and the DA's 71.
  2. The ANC alone has the most seats in 45% of simulations and the DA alone in 47%. Two or more parties tie for the most seats in 4.0%. Everything else, MK included, shares what is left.
  3. The complaint about polls does not reach this forecast. This Model uses no poll at all. It is built from the record — how this city has voted, and the trajectories we can measure in that record. So the claim that pollsters undercount MK, whether or not it is right, leaves these numbers where they are. To be largest, MK would have to be under-represented in past ballot boxes, not in a survey — a different and much harder argument.
23 October 2026: what This Model says now

Since then MK is the largest party in 3.7% of simulations. Its median is 19 seats and the top of its range 57, against the ANC's 72 and the DA's 71.

26 September 2026: Published · Link to this claim
Democratic AllianceHelen Zille, DA · Daily Maverick, 13 September 2026
We won't go into a coalition with MK, and we will not go into coalition with the EFF. Because our value set is too incompatible. You cannot govern with people who don't share any of your ideology”
A majority without the ANC, the EFF and MK is possible for the DAVERY LIKELY

A red line is a choice, so there is nothing here to call true or false. What can be tested is what it costs. Without the ANC, the EFF and MK, the rest of the council still clears 136 in 88% of simulations.

  1. There are three ways to a majority. The whole rest of the council except the ANC, which clears 136 in 88% of simulations; a deal with the ANC alone, 67%; or the narrower option, ActionSA and the PA, 8.6%.
  2. The first assumes the whole rest of the council acting as one, ActionSA, the PA and several small parties: the most generous and least stable option.
  3. The DA and the ANC together clear 136 in 67% of simulations. Neither party has said it wants it, but on this arithmetic it is the most reliable two-party majority in the city.
  4. The DA with ActionSA and the Patriotic Alliance reaches 136 in 8.6% of simulations. Across all simulations the three hold a median of 118 seats; where they reach a majority, a median of 140.
  5. The same test applied to the other side: an ANC-led council excluding the DA, the EFF and MK reaches 136 in 78% of simulations.
23 October 2026: what This Model says now

Since then the rest of the council, without the ANC, the EFF and MK, clears 136 in 88% of simulations. The DA and the ANC together clear it in 67%; the DA with ActionSA and the PA in 8.6%. The claim holds with a 88% chance, which the scale calls very likely. Correction, 23 October 2026: step 1 called the first route "the whole rest of the council except the ANC". Its figure is the rest of the council without the ANC, the EFF and MK together.

7 October 2026: Published · Link to this claim
ActionSAHerman Mashaba, ActionSA leader and mayoral candidate, of Helen Zille (DA) · Inside Politics, 9 September 2026
If she thinks she will win Joburg without the support of Soweto, she must be living in another world”
The DA cannot win Joburg without SowetoQUITE LIKELY

No party is likely to win a majority on its own: under 0.1% chance. So winning Joburg means the DA leading a coalition of at least 136 of 270 seats. Zille has ruled out the EFF and MK. That leaves three realistic partners: ActionSA with the PA, or the ANC. In 2021 the DA won no Soweto ward and governed only through a 9-party coalition that fell within a year.

  1. Mathematically, the DA can lead a coalition in 87% of simulations. Most of these depend on many parties staying together, any of which can walk out and bring the coalition down. We do not count them.
  2. In over 99% of simulations the DA wins none of the 41 Soweto wards. Without a Soweto ward it can still govern in 36% of simulations, with ActionSA and the PA or with the ANC: 8.3% and 33% respectively (some simulations allow either).
  3. In the 0.7% of simulations where the DA does win a Soweto ward, its chance of governing jumps to 89%. These simulations show a strong DA across the whole city: the breakthrough the DA has never achieved, and needs to make inroads.
  4. Mashaba is probably correct. The DA needs Sowetan support, or partners who have it.
  5. What this cannot see. This Model counts seats, not which parties are willing to work together.
23 October 2026: what This Model says now

Since then the forecast reads the IEC's September roll, and this site counts 41 wards as Soweto. The DA wins none of them in over 99% of simulations. Without one it governs in 36%: with ActionSA and the PA in 8.3%, with the ANC in 33%. The claim holds with a 64% chance, which the scale calls quite likely. Correction, 23 October 2026: no ward was added to Soweto. Ward 29 now counts as Soweto because this site now places each voting district wholly in one ward. The report quoted is Inside Politics', of an interview with Inside Metros; the card named Inside Metros. The 89% in the third step rests on 37 of the 5,000 simulations, too few to give a chance.
