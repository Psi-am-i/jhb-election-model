What changed, and what it did

ActionSA is held at 17.6% of the list vote

The model's own figure for ActionSA was 12.6%. It starts from ActionSA's 2021 list vote, 18.1%, and keeps 72% of it, the typical share a party of its size keeps from one local election to the next, because ActionSA has stood in only one. Four by-elections since 2021 raise it to 14.7%, a pull toward the average size takes it to 14.5%, and making every party's share add to 100% brings it to 12.6%. The owner set it at 17.6%. Every other party's share is scaled down by the same factor, 0.943, so the shares still add to 100%. ActionSA keeps the model's usual uncertainty for a party of that size: its 90% range is 13.3% to 22.3% (was 8.4% to 17.9%).

ActionSA: 40.5 seats, from 29.3

Its 90% range is 31 to 51 seats (was 20 to 41). It wins almost no wards in either model, so its seats come from the list.

The DA loses more seats than the ANC

The DA falls from 71.0 seats to 66.7 and the ANC from 70.0 to 69.2, although both lose the same 5.7% of their list vote. The ANC wins more wards than its list vote entitles it to: in 86% of simulations (90% with the call) its seats equal its ward wins, 68 of them on average, so a lower list vote does not reduce them. The DA wins 45 wards and takes the rest of its seats from the list, so its seats follow its list vote. The ANC is now the largest party in 60% of simulations (was 46%), the DA in 36% (was 49%); a tie for most seats is split evenly between the tied parties. No party wins a majority in any simulation of either model.

Wards: one changes favourite

Ward 124 was a tie between the DA and MK at 28% each; it now leans MK, 28% to 26%.

Past elections: unchanged

A party call applies only to an election that has not been held, so the backtest over 24 past metro elections is the same in both models.

What the call rests on

It is a judgement, not a measurement: ActionSA has one local election on record. The evidence either way is its 2021 list vote (18.1%), its share of Johannesburg's 2024 national ballot (6.22%, which the model does not use for ActionSA), and four by-elections since 2021 that imply 18.9%. After the election, ActionSA's result is checked against both 17.6% and the model's own 12.6%.

Both models are the code at commit 8ce7372, run at 5,000 simulations. The site still shows the 29 September forecast.
