Taken together

The DA and the ANC are level, and the ranges are narrower

Johannesburg 2026, average seats: ANC 63.7 to 70.0, DA 66.5 to 71.0, ActionSA 28.4 to 29.3, EFF 27.2 to 27.7, MK 24.2 to 23.4, PA 24.0 to 17.3. The DA is the largest party in 49% of simulations (was 46%), the ANC in 46% (was 47%), MK in 4% (was 1%); a tie for most seats is split evenly between the tied parties. No party wins a majority in any simulation.

The ranges narrow for every large party except MK: the ANC's 90% range goes from 28–92 seats to 50–81, the DA's from 45–90 to 55–88, the EFF's from 8–57 to 16–44. MK's widens, from 7–50 to 6–57.

Twelve wards change favourite. The PA loses five (29, 69, 121, 124, 125), MK loses six (37, 40, 59, 63, 65, 123), and ward 92 goes from the DA to the ANC.

Past elections

Each model forecast the 2011, 2016 and 2021 elections in the eight metros, using only data from before each election. Across the eight metros, today's model against the 29 September one: in 2016, wards called wrong 25 to 25 and seats given to the wrong party 72 to 64; in 2021, wards called wrong 23 to 24 and seats to the wrong party 131 to 132; in 2011, wards called wrong 23 to 29 and seats to the wrong party 164.5 to 159. Average list-vote error per metro: 2016 0.99 to 1.10 points, 2021 2.92 to 2.99, 2011 5.57 to 5.57.

Johannesburg alone: 2016 wards called wrong 1 to 1, seats to the wrong party 15 to 14; 2021 wards wrong 8 to 8, seats to the wrong party 43 to 45.

The two by-election changes (12 and 13) move none of these figures: no past election has by-elections before it in the record. Checked by re-running after change 13; every figure came out identical.

What changed since 29 September

1. The PA's ward vote is no longer inflated

Each party's ward vote is scaled to the wards it contests. The PA's city-wide ward vote falls from 11.9% to 6.5%, against a list vote of 6.3%. 14 October report.

2. A new way of varying the vote in each simulation

All parties are varied the same way, scaled to their size, by an amount set from how far past results strayed. This is what narrows the big parties' ranges. 14 October report.

3. The IEC's 2026 voters' roll

The forecast uses the IEC's roll of 26 September 2026, ward by ward, instead of the 2024 roll redrawn onto the new wards. 15 October report.

4. Each voting district counts in one ward

As the IEC counts it, so every ward's total matches the IEC's. 16 October report.

5. A party with its own national record is placed from it

MK, RISE and BOSA are placed voting district by voting district from their own 2024 results, not from an assumed share of the ANC's geography. 16 October report.

6. Candidates standing only for a ward get no city-wide votes

Ward-only parties get no list votes, and every party wins only the wards it stands in. 16 October report.

7. Small parties keep their own geography

A fault had flattened every small party's vote to the same minimum everywhere. 16 October report.

8. The council always adds to 270

16 October report.

9. Past councils are scored with their independents

Changes the scoring, not the model. 16 October report.

10. New parties get one of two widths

A party standing for the first time is varied more if it had no vote in the city at the last national election than if it had. 16 October (turnout) report.

11. Registered voters split by each ward's census shares

The turnout change of 16 October was reverted; this replaced the old step-by-step population estimate. 17 October report.

12. By-elections count half, for every party

Your decision of 17 October. Each party's expected share is set halfway between the model's expectation from earlier elections and what the by-elections since 2021 imply. On 14 October by-elections had been set to count for nothing. On its own this moved the ANC's chance of being the largest party alone from 57.0% to 47.4% and the DA's from 35.4% to 44.3%. It has not been tested against any past election: no past election in the record has by-elections before it.

13. A party with no earlier record is not moved by its by-elections

Your decision of 19 October. Under change 12 the All Citizens Party went from 0.05% to 1.24% of the vote on two by-elections (159 and 39 votes). A party with no vote in the 2024 national election and none in the 2021 local election now keeps its own size: All Citizens is back at 0.05%. Four parties are affected; MK keeps its by-elections.

One queued correction to a comment in Johannesburg's settings file goes in at the same time, because any change to that file forces the rebuild. The rebuild should move no number on this page; it is checked by re-running.

The party sheet judgements. Two MK confirmations. How the voters of parties that split off are divided. Whether and how polls enter the forecast (none does now).

By-elections: what could still change

The IEC's by-elections from 2016 to 2021 are now downloaded: 319 contests, 77 of them in the metros. With them the half weight can be tested against the 2021 election, and so can your idea that a by-election that changed the seat should count for more (three of Johannesburg's 15 contests since 2021 changed the seat, all to the PA). Either test could change the weight. Neither is run yet.

Known gaps

Tshwane's by-elections are not read, so its forecast ignores them. The forecast should not be used for turnout scenarios (16 October finding). If the 2024 national results file were missing when the inputs are rebuilt, MK would be treated as a party with no record; it is present now, and a check for it is queued for the rebuild.

The 29 September model is the code published then, re-run at 5,000 simulations; its averages match the published forecast to within half a seat. Today's model is the code at commit a9754ed.
