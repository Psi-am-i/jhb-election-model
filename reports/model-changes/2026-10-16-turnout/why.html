What changed, and what it did

Taken together: the ANC's lead over the DA widens by 1.2 seats

Johannesburg 2026, average seats: ANC 71.7 to 72.5, DA 69.8 to 68.9, EFF 30.3 to 30.3, ActionSA 27.2 to 27.3, MK 23.4 to 23.6, PA 15.8 to 15.5. The ANC is the largest party in 62% of simulations (was 56%), the DA in 34% (was 38%), MK in 4% (was 6%); a tie for most seats is split evenly between the tied parties. One ward changes favourite: ward 82, PA to DA, both near even. Ranges narrow for MK (4 to 64 seats becomes 6 to 56), the EFF (18 to 46 becomes 21 to 42) and ActionSA (17 to 40 becomes 20 to 36).

1. A new party's spread depends on whether it has a national result

A party standing in a city for the first time has no local record, so its range is wider than an established party's. The old model used one width for all of them. The new model uses two, each fitted on past elections: one for parties that already won votes in the city at the last national election (MK, RISE, BOSA in 2026), and one for parties that did not. You chose this on 16 October.

Effect: MK's range narrows (4 to 64 seats becomes 6 to 57) and its chance of being the largest party falls from 5.8% to 3.8%. Average seats move by under 0.4. Nothing in the past elections moves: 2011 and 2016 have too few such parties to fit two widths, and in 2021 the two widths change no ward call and move seats to the wrong party by at most half a seat. On the 2021 record the new widths predict parties with a national result better and parties without one slightly worse.

2. Turnout now changes the vote

The old model drew a turnout for each group of voters, but then rescaled so the city-wide vote came out the same whatever turnout was drawn: turnout moved votes between wards and changed no party's city-wide share. In the new model the city-wide vote is the sum of what each voting district casts at its drawn turnout, so a group that turns out less counts for less. You decided this on 16 October; the starting point is the model's own expected turnout for each group.

Effect: the drawn turnouts are skewed, and that skew now reaches the vote. The DA's average list share falls from 26.1% to 25.8% and the ANC's rises from 23.8% to 23.9%; ANC seats 72.0 to 72.5, DA 69.7 to 68.9. Spreads change: the DA's widens, the EFF's and ActionSA's narrow.

What it did to the past elections

All of it comes from change 2. Across the eight metros: in 2016 one more ward is called right (Johannesburg) and seats to the wrong party are unchanged; in 2021 wards called are unchanged and seats to the wrong party fall by 3; in 2011 wards called are unchanged and seats to the wrong party rise by 4. Average list-vote error falls in 2016 (1.10 to 0.99 points) and 2021 (2.98 to 2.89) and rises slightly in 2011 (5.56 to 5.57). The largest improvement is Nelson Mandela Bay (2016: 1.10 to 0.57 points); the largest worsening is Tshwane 2016 (1.25 to 1.42).

What the new turnout mechanism does not yet get right

As a test, we gave the new model each past election's real turnout instead of its own expected turnout. The forecasts got worse, not better: average list-vote error rose from 3.15 to 3.63 points and was better at only 6 of 24 past elections, though 5 more wards were called right. Real turnout fell less in DA-strong areas than the model expected, and the DA's real share did not rise with it. So the mechanism moves the vote in the right direction for the right reason, but by too much: if turnout falls in mainly Black African areas, the model gives the DA more than past elections support. Two explanations fit and our data cannot yet separate them: people who stay home vote differently from their neighbours who vote, or the model's starting point already includes local-election turnout and the turnout effect is counted twice. Until this is settled, the forecast should not be used for turnout scenarios.

What we predicted, and what it got wrong

Written before the run. Held: the DA's average list share falls and the ANC's rises at most past elections (20 of 24), each within 0.25 points of our calculation; list-vote error does not worsen overall (3.21 to 3.15). Held: MK's chance of being the largest party lands between 1.5% and 4.5% (3.8%).

Wrong: we predicted that real turnout would improve the forecasts. It worsened them (above). We predicted it would change at least 10 ward calls; it changed 5 on balance. We predicted the new newcomer widths would score no worse on parties without a national result, beyond chance; they scored worse by more than chance. We predicted the newcomer change would move the 2011 and 2016 elections most; it moved only 2021.
