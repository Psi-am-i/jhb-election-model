How the forecast is made

Draft for the owner, 15 October 2026. The forecast is how often each outcome happens across 5,000 simulations of the Johannesburg 2026 local election. Worked examples use real parties and real voting districts.

The forecast in four steps

  1. 1. City-wide levelEach party gets a 2026 city-wide share of the vote. It starts from the party's latest results and how its national vote has converted into local vote in the past.
  2. 2. PlacementThat city-wide share is spread across Johannesburg's voting districts and wards. A party keeps the shape of its most recent map: where it ran ahead of its city average, it stays ahead.
  3. 3. VariationThe election is simulated 5,000 times. Each simulation moves every party's city-wide share, the turnout in every voting district and every party's tally in every ward by a random amount.
  4. 4. SeatsEach simulation's votes become a council. Seats follow combined ward and list votes: a party's ward wins count against the seats its combined vote entitles it to.

The ANC's 2026 city-wide level is set from two routes and a shrink

The model estimates a party's 2026 share two ways and blends them. The national route takes the party's 2024 national-ballot result in Johannesburg and multiplies it by the ratio between its national and local results in past cycles. The local route takes its 2021 local result and multiplies it by the ratio between consecutive local results. Both ratios are averages over past elections, pulled toward the value typical for a party of that size.

The model has no trend term. A party's decline from one election to the next does not carry forward as momentum; only the latest results and the average past conversion ratios enter. The straight lines are shown for comparison and are not used. A recency-weighted version of one related quantity, the total vote going to parties standing for the first time, was tested on the 2016 and 2021 elections and did not score better than a plain average of past elections. That test has two elections behind it. The owner's proposal to add momentum and acceleration terms (4 September 2026) is recorded and not started.

MK's vote in two voting districts: Dube Hostel Hall and Bryanston

A party that stood in 2024 is placed by its own 2024 map. In each voting district the model measures MK's lean: the gap between its share there and its city-wide share, taken on a scale (log-odds) that keeps every share between 0% and 100%. That lean carries into 2026 in full. The whole map is then scaled by one multiplier so the districts add up to MK's 2026 city-wide total. Finally, each district's party shares are divided by their sum so they total 100%. The same lean is used on the ward and list ballots.

MK's 2024 city-wide share was 12.22% (172,958 votes, national ballot). The example uses 8.99%, MK's average city-wide share across the 5,000 simulations. Reaching it takes a multiplier of 0.729 on the 2024 share, giving MK a base level of 8.90%.

The population-group mix does not place MK's votes

The voter-mix row is not used in the arithmetic above. For a party with a 2024 record, the population-group mix only books its city-wide votes to the four groups. That booking decides which group's turnout swing moves MK's city-wide total in a given simulation. It does not move MK up or down in any district.

The model's current setting books 87.2% of MK's votes to Black African voters. Without the owner's setting it would book 68.6%. Across paired 5,000-simulation runs the two give the same average MK share (8.99%), the same average seats (23.30) and average ward wins of 10.28 and 10.29. Simulation by simulation, MK's city-wide share differs between the two by a standard deviation of 0.21 points. The largest difference in any district's average share is 0.023 points.

Two ecological estimates, which infer who voted for a party from how its vote rises and falls with each district's voter mix, put MK's 2024 Johannesburg vote at 81-94% Black African. The owner has chosen to fit MK's group mix from its own 2024 results; that change is queued. A group mix places a party on the map only when it has no 2024 record: a new party, or one that stands only in local elections.

The forecast starts from the previous ward map because group arithmetic misses wards

An alternative to the previous map is to build each ward from the bottom up: count each population group in the ward, apply each group's turnout and party support, and add. This check ran that recipe against two counted elections on the 2021 ward map (135 wards): the 2019 national ballot and the 2021 local list ballot.

The census White group and the roll's White group are different populations

To match the 2021 results, the fit needs 1.74 registered White voters for every White adult the census counts. A rate above 1 is impossible, so the White group in the model is not White voters only. About 43% of its estimated registrations are people the census does not count as White adults. Any recipe that starts from census population carries this mismatch.

ANC average across simulations: 23.67%. The draws are not symmetric for every party: MK's middle simulation gives it 6.7% against an average of 9.0%, because some simulations give MK much more.

The shape of the map does not vary systematically. No term moves a party up in one area and down in another; only the random district turnout and ward factors do that. A party that gains city-wide gains in every district in proportion to its lean. Polls are switched off and by-elections carry zero weight in the 2026 forecast, so neither reshapes the map.

The voters' roll is the IEC's 26 September 2026 roll

The forecast reads the IEC roll of 26 September 2026: 2,417,915 registered voters. The roll published on 29 September was identical. The IEC counts each voting district wholly in one ward. The previous roll used planners' estimated splits of districts that straddle ward boundaries, which put ward 60 at 14,810; the IEC count is 25,231. An assignment of each district to one ward was solved so that every ward's total matches the IEC's. As a check, compare each ward's 2026 IEC count with the 2024 registration of the districts assigned to it: with the planners' splits that ratio ranged from 0.70 to 1.70 across wards (standard deviation 0.168); with the solved assignment it ranges from 0.82 to 1.27 (standard deviation 0.060), close to what real registration growth looks like. About 17 districts cannot be placed from the files available; those wards carry the remaining error.

The roll changes where votes are, not city-wide shares, which are set first. With the new roll, 2 ward favourites changed, then 5 after the specs were rebuilt; no party moved by as much as 1 seat.

Where an independent topped the poll, the typical winning margin was about 4 points. In 9 of 10 such cases the independent beat the ANC in a ward the ANC had dominated, with about 76% of the vote before. An independent's earlier showing in a ward does not predict a win. In 6 of 9 cases the independent vote was zero at the next election. The files are consistent with splits in the dominant party's local base; they carry no candidate names, so that reading is an interpretation, not a measurement.

The rule being built: a ward-only candidate gets no list votes and can win only the wards it contests. Its vote is placed first by which ward it stands in, then by who lives there, preferably by which party those voters backed before.

What the forecast cannot see

  • Trends. There is no trend term. A party in steady decline is forecast from its latest results and past conversion ratios, not from the direction of travel.
  • Changes in the map's shape. Each party keeps its most recent map. Only random district turnout and ward factors move it. A party that gains in one region and loses in another between elections is not anticipated.
  • Polls. Switched off in the 2026 forecast.
  • By-elections. Weighted at zero in the 2026 forecast.
  • New parties. A party with no 2024 record is placed by population-group arithmetic only, the method the ward check above scores worse than the previous map.
  • Independents. Not yet modelled. The ward-only rule above is being built.
