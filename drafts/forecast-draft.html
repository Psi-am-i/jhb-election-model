Before the numbers

How to read this page

This forecast is not one prediction — it is 5,000 simulations of the election, each drawing a plausible 2026 within the bounds of everything observed since 2006. Every percentage on this page means the same thing: the share of those 5,000 simulations in which something happens. "89%" never means a vote share or a poll number — it means the thing occurred in about 4,450 of the 5,000 runs. The ranges beside each seat count are where the middle 90% of simulations landed.

The rule that shapes everything

Wards, lists — and the clause that fires in November

Johannesburg votes on two ballots at once: one elects a ward councillor, race by race, and one is for a party. It seems like they elect two separate halves of the council, but they don't — the two ballots are added together, and that combined share sets the total seats each party is entitled to. Winning a ward doesn't add a seat; it fills one the party was already owed, and the party lists top everyone up to their entitlement.

The quirk: a party with tightly concentrated support can win more wards than its overall share entitles it to. Nobody unseats a ward winner — the seat law's "excessive seats" clause (Schedule 1, item 16, completed by a 2021 amendment) settles it: the winner keeps every ward, receives no list seats at all, and the other parties' list seats are recalculated over what remains. The council stays at 270 and the majority at 136 — we found the Electoral Commission applying exactly this in Laingsburg in 2021. It is now the ANC's normal condition: roughly 73 stronghold wards on a 65-seat entitlement, in six of every seven simulations. Its ward count is an unbreakable floor — and the parties squeezed to pay for it are everyone else.

The third way to govern

A minority government, resting on abstention

No coalition table shows it, but the mayor is elected by councillors present and voting, so abstention installs minority governments — it is how the DA took this mayoralty in 2021. If only the ANC votes against, a DA minority administration is arithmetically viable in — of simulations. The realistic outcomes are three: a grand coalition, a DA-led minority resting on abstention, or a DA arrangement with one of the parties it has ruled out.

Design choices

Why the model is built the way it is

Seats, not vote shares

The council follows a statutory formula, not a percentage. The model's allocator reproduces the real 2011, 2016 and 2021 councils exactly — quota, vote totals, every party's seats — so the formula layer is not an assumption.

Geography is the anchor

Tested across election cycles: where a party's support sits transfers almost perfectly; how much support it has does not. So the model fits the geography once and treats each party's level as an explicit, bounded scenario — the honest shape of the uncertainty.

Parties move in blocs

The DA and ActionSA fight over the same voters, as do the ANC, EFF and MK. Drawing blocs first and splitting within them is why DA+ActionSA's combined range is far tighter than ActionSA's own 10–50: their errors offset. Ignoring that correlation produces impossible scenarios.

Turnout was tested — and demoted

The model was built expecting differential turnout to decide the city. Measured directly, a ten-point swing across thirty wards moves about two seats — one, under the stricter marginal-voter test. The gap to a majority is over thirty. Persuasion, not mobilisation, is the only route.

Validation

How it was checked — and how you can

How it was checked

Shown only 2021's citywide totals — nothing about where the votes sat — the model placed them well enough to call 129 of 135 ward winners. Fully blind, its seat error quintuples: the spatial machinery is validated, the citywide ranges are judgements. That split is why the output is a distribution.

Limits

What this forecast cannot tell you

  • Viability is not intent. The 87% for ANC+DA is arithmetic, not a prediction that it happens. The model says which deals are available; it says nothing about whether people who dislike each other will sit in the same room.
  • It cannot see a party that does not yet exist. ActionSA went from absent to 44 seats in one cycle. The entrant slot in the simulations is an honest patch, not a fix.
  • The ranges are the output. A single seat number quoted from this sheet without its range is a misuse of it. The probabilities are exactly as good as the stated assumptions — which are all adjustable, above.
  • The overhang rule awaits a worked example. Council expansion follows the Municipal Structures Act as read; the IEC has never published a metro overhang calculation. With overhang now the modal outcome, this is the forecast's largest single legal uncertainty.
  • It is not a poll. No voters were surveyed. This is arithmetic on results, registration, and fifteen by-elections.
