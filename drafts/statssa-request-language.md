# Data request to Stats SA — home language by ward, and a follow-up on the SAL

**Status:** draft, not sent. To go to `info@statssa.gov.za` (+27 12 310 8600).
A first request for the Small Area Layer was sent **2026-08-05** and has not
been answered; this both follows that up and adds a second, probably easier
ask.

Two separate asks in one message, because the second is likely far quicker to
grant than the first and we would rather not have it held up behind it.

---

## Suggested subject

Census 2022: home language by ward (metros), and follow-up on a Small Area
Layer request of 5 August

## Suggested body

Dear Statistics South Africa,

I am building an open, publicly published statistical model of the 2026
municipal elections in South Africa's metropolitan municipalities. The model,
its code, its sources and its errors are all published openly, and Stats SA
data is credited and linked throughout. Two requests, the second of which is
a follow-up.

**1. Census 2022 home language, by ward**

Your Ward Statistical Product (the locked spreadsheets released 27 October
2025) has been extremely useful — we are using `Population Group`, `Age Group`
and `Sex` at ward level for all 4,000-odd wards.

We would like the equivalent table for **home language**: counts by ward, in
the same shape as `PP_Population Group_27-10-2025.xlsx` — a `Ward_Code` column
and one column per language category, with a total.

National coverage would be ideal so that no further request is needed per
municipality, but if that is difficult, the eight metropolitan municipalities
alone would be enough: Johannesburg, Tshwane, Ekurhuleni, Cape Town,
eThekwini, Nelson Mandela Bay, Mangaung and Buffalo City.

If a language table already exists in the Ward Product series and we have
simply missed it, a pointer would be just as welcome as a file.

*Why we are asking:* population group alone turns out to be too coarse to
describe how voters are grouped. Two parties can both draw overwhelmingly
from one population group and yet have entirely separate geographic bases,
which strongly suggests a linguistic rather than a racial grouping. Without
language at ward level we cannot test that, and we would rather state a
limitation than publish an inference we cannot support.

**2. Follow-up: Census 2022 Small Area Layer**

On 5 August we requested the Small Area Layer for income, dwelling type and
employment, which we understand is supplied on request rather than published.
We have not had a reply and appreciate that the request may simply be in a
queue. If there is a form, a fee, a data-sharing agreement or a different
address we should be using, please say so and we will follow whatever process
is required.

If the SAL cannot be released, ward-level equivalents of any of income,
employment status or dwelling type would still be very valuable, and we would
be glad to take a coarser product rather than none.

We are happy to sign any conditions of use, to credit Stats SA in a specific
form, or to share what we build with you before publication.

With thanks,

*[name, affiliation, contact]*

## Notes for whoever sends this

* Attach or name nothing confidential — everything in this project is public.
* If they ask what the data is for in more detail, the honest one-line answer
  is: *to decide how many distinct voter groupings a city has, and to put a
  demographic bound on how large a new party can get, instead of guessing.*
* The language ask is the higher priority of the two. Income would be nice;
  language is currently the binding constraint on the model's structure.
* Worth trying the **provincial Stats SA office** as well if the national
  address stays quiet — the ward product came out of the same series.
