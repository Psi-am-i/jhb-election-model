Where the parties land

The headline is not the DA's lead. It is the arithmetic underneath it. Johannesburg elects half its council on a ward ballot and half on a party-list ballot, and a party's total entitlement is set by both added together. That makes the council close to proportional — and it means a fragmented vote produces a fragmented council that no natural alliance can assemble into 136.

A majority without the ANC exists on paper. It requires almost every other party in the chamber at once. The Patriotic Alliance — projected at 11 seats and the largest party outside the two blocs — matters more to that arithmetic than ActionSA's recovery does.

A finding that cuts against the conventional read

Turnout will not decide this

The model was built expecting differential turnout to be decisive — that a collapse in ANC-leaning turnout in the south would hand the city to a DA-led bloc. Tested directly, it does not. Moving turnout by ten points across the thirty most ANC-leaning wards shifts roughly two seats. DA + ActionSA need thirty-one.

Turnout still shapes who wins individual wards. It does not close a gap of that size. On this arithmetic the route to 136 runs through persuading voters, not through opponents staying home.

How it works

Each of the 5,000 runs draws a citywide result for 2026, projects it onto all 865 voting districts using each party's historic local geography, applies projected turnout, then runs the statutory seat formula — quota, division, largest remainder.

How it was checked

The seat calculator reproduces the officially published Johannesburg council exactly for 2011, 2016 and 2021 — quota, vote totals and every party's seat count. Tested against 2021 without being shown the answer, the model called 128 of 135 ward winners.

What this forecast cannot tell you

  • It cannot see a party that does not yet exist. ActionSA was not on the ballot in 2019 and won 44 seats in 2021. Nothing here would have predicted that, and nothing here would predict an equivalent arrival in 2026.
  • It is a distribution, not a prediction. The ranges are the output. A single seat number taken from this sheet without its range is a misuse of it.
  • It assumes the contest resembles recent ones. The inputs are informed judgements about how much each party's support shifts between national and local elections, not certainties.
  • It is not a poll. No voters were surveyed. This is arithmetic on past results and registration data.
