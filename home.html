Proportional representation is designed to give each party seats that match their support. Direct representation lets voters elect a specific person to represent them. Our local government elections are a mix of both: we directly elect ward councillors and then each party is allocated additional seats so that each party's percentage of the council matches their percentage of the vote. There is an edge case where this doesn't happen.

A ward you win cannot be taken away, so a party that wins many wards can end up with more council seats than its share of the vote should allow. How this is built
How This Model works
The simulations are built from how every voting district in the city has voted before.
Where the data comes from
Each source file and the workarounds it needed. The files' checksums are in the project's repository.
Two ballots, one council
The ward and party ballots are added together to set each party's seats. One clause covers a party that wins more wards than its share of the vote entitles it to.
How well it has done
This Model was scored on the 2021 election in eight cities, against the results and three simple baselines.
How This Model is tested
Each version is reviewed against its own plan and its errors fixed. This is the review of the first version.
What this forecast cannot tell you
It cannot say which deal will be made, and it may underestimate a new party.
How to read the page
A chance is the share of the {{n_draws}} simulations in which the outcome happens, not a share of the vote.
What this is

An unaffiliated forecast of Johannesburg's council election, built from primary sources. It starts from each voting district's history: past results, turnout, census data and each party's trajectory. From there it builds the city up from its wards, rather than applying generalisations down.

This Model simulates the election thousands of times. Each simulation uses different values for turnout and for every party's support, each drawn from how far turnout and parties have moved in past elections.

It uses as few judgement calls as possible. Each one is listed, with its evidence, on the judgement calls page, and several can be changed on your prediction.
